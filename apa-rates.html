APA crew rates

The current APA recommended rate for UK commercial crew, and when it next changes. These are the figures TimeMachine applies to your days.

Which card is current

The card in force is the APA Recommended Crew Terms, September 2025. It runs until 31 August 2026. The next card, September 2026, takes effect on 1 September 2026. The APA reviews and republishes the rates once a year, always from 1 September.

From day rate to hourly rate

Two numbers drive everything. Your basic day rate is what you agree with production. Your basic hourly rate is exactly one tenth of it, not one eleventh, even though a basic day is eleven hours. Double time is twice the hourly rate, triple is three times it, and overtime is the hourly rate multiplied by a coefficient. Once the day rate is set, every other figure follows from it. There is more on this in how APA rates actually work.

The three overtime grades

Each role sits in one of three overtime grades, and the grade sets the multiplier: time and a half, time and a quarter, or flat time with no uplift. The card states which grade each role is in, and as a rule the higher the day rate, the lower the multiplier. There is more on how the grades work in how APA overtime actually works. Directors and producers have no overtime grade at all.

The overtime column shows each role's standard coefficient. Directors and producers have no overtime. Production managers, assistants and runners are shown at × 1, but their overtime is not a single multiplier: it is your basic hourly rate on a weekday, one and a half times it on a Saturday, and twice it on a Sunday or bank holiday.

Where the figures come from

Almost every row here is the APA card rate. Two are marked † because they are not: Best Boy and Lighting Trainee are TimeMachine defaults, not card grades. Best Boy's figure matches the card's Programmable Lighting Desk Op grade exactly on both cards, £512 then £527, and the card has no separate Best Boy grade. Lighting Trainee is not an APA grade at all, and its £250 is a TimeMachine default. Rigging is a third case, just below: those rows show the card rate, but the app applies the higher Bectu figures.

Rigging

The two rigging rows in the table above are the APA card figures, both Grade I, and they rise with the 2026 card like the rest. Riggers on commercials generally work to the higher Bectu rate instead, and that is what TimeMachine applies: Master Rigger £675, Advanced Rigger £515 and Standby Rigger £515. Mind the overlap in names: an Advanced Rigger on the card is the rate shown above, but on Bectu an Advanced Rigger is £515, and it is the Bectu figure the app uses.

For the full card and anything not shown, go to the APA's published Recommended Crew Terms.
