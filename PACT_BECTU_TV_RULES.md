# Pact/Bectu Agreement — Terms And Conditions For Engaging Crew On Scripted TV

**Effective:** 1 January 2023
**Parties:** Pact (producers) and Bectu (workers)
**Signatories:** Max Rumney (Pact), Spencer MacDonald (Bectu)
**Minimum term:** 3 years to 1 January 2026, then 6 months' written notice to terminate either side
**Source:** `pact-bectu-scripted-tv-agreement-sig-copy-12-december-2022.pdf` (union.bectu.org.uk, signed copy), plus Joint Pact/Bectu Guidance issued 8 December 2023 — this is a markdown rebuild of the same content for easier reference

> **Three separate rulebooks now.** This is the **scripted TV** agreement. See `PACT_BECTU_FILM_RULES.md` for feature films and `APA_RULES.md` for commercials. The maths is different in all three — see [Differences At A Glance](#differences-at-a-glance).

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

### Day types — shooting day

All three are **paid as 10 Contracted Hours**. What differs is the elapsed clock and the break.

| Day type | Shooting hrs | Break | Elapsed from call | OT starts after | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard Working Day (SWD) | 10 | 1 hr unpaid lunch | 11h | 11h | 8am–7pm |
| Semi-Continuous Working Day (SCWD) | 9.5 | 30 min unpaid lunch | 10h | 10h | 8am–6pm |
| Continuous Working Day (CWD) | 9 | 20 min rest, no cessation of work | 9h | 9h | 8am–5pm |

**Add 1 hour to each of the above** for workers on the Additional Contracted Hour (see below): SWD 12h, SCWD 11h, CWD 10h.

### Contracted Hours — 10+1 vs 10+1+1

| | Contracted Hours | Departments |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 10 + 1 | All departments not listed below |
| With Additional Contracted Hour (ACH) | 10 + 1 + 1 | Assistant Directors, Costume, Hair & Make-up (and Prosthetics), Locations, Production, Script Supervisors |

- **Basic Daily Rate (BDR)** = contractual rate for a 10 hour day
- **Hourly Rate (HR)** = BDR ÷ 10
- **Daily Rate** = the 10+1 or 10+1+1 rate as applicable
- **ACH** is paid at 1 × HR, is **guaranteed even if not worked**, **cannot be aggregated across the week**, and **is not overtime**

*Example:* BDR £250 → HR £25 → ACH £25 → Total Daily Rate £275.

### Overtime rates

| Situation | Rate |
|---|---|
| Standard overtime | HR × **1.5T** |
| After 11pm | HR × **2T** |
| UK Bank Holiday | HR × **2T** |
| Seventh consecutive day | HR × **2T** |
| Sixth consecutive day (shooting or not) | standard OT rate (1.5T) |

**Floor and ceiling — apply to all of the above, including 2T and all penalties:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum Overtime Rate | **£35/hr**, pro-rated for partial hours |
| Maximum Overtime Rate | **£70/hr**, or the Worker's HR at 1T if that is greater, pro-rated |

- OT accrues in **15 minute increments**, shooting and non-shooting, **rounded up** — Section D Examples 4 and 5 confirm 1h10m bills as 1h15m, 1h20m as 1h30m, and 25m as 30m
- Each situation giving rise to an OT-rate payment **accrues separately and may not be aggregated across the working day**
- No OT arises where a Worker works fewer than their Contracted Hours
- Nothing that generates a payment may be worked unless requested by the Producer or pre-approved by the Producer's Nominated Representative

**Where the caps bite:** BDR below ~£233 hits the £35 floor. HR above £70 (BDR above £700) means the cap becomes HR × 1T rather than 1.5T.

### Rates that are *not* overtime

| Item | Rate | Rounding | Capped by Max OT Rate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Call (work before 06:00) | +0.5T, so **1.5T** total | up to nearest 15 min | **No** |
| Broken Turnaround (worked inside 11h turnaround) | +0.5T, so **1.5T** total | up to nearest 15 min | Not stated as OT |
| Night Work (scheduled shooting past 11pm) | +1T on top, so **2T** total, *or* comp rest | up to nearest 15 min | Not stated as OT |

### Consecutive days

| Day | Shooting | Non-shooting |
|---|---|---|
| 6th consecutive | **1.5T** | ≤6h worked: BDR at **1T**. >6h worked: BDR at **1.5T**. Requires prior written approval or nothing is payable |
| 6th consecutive on a UK Bank Holiday | **2T** | **2T** |
| 7th consecutive | **2T** (Producers will not schedule 7 consecutive *filming* days) | **2T** |
| 8th consecutive | **Strictly prohibited** | **Strictly prohibited** |

Travel days do not count towards sixth and seventh consecutive day calculations.

### Premiums

| Premium | Trigger | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Split Day | Camera finish between 9pm and 11pm, beyond the worker's cap | **£30** per day |
| 11-day fortnight | Sixth days beyond the applicable cap | **£100** per day |

**Split Day caps:** engaged 0–7 weeks — no cap. Engaged over 7 weeks — 40% of total filming days in the overall shooting schedule. Reconciled and paid at end of contract.

**11-day fortnight caps (sixth days):**

| Shoot weeks | Cap |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | 4 |
| 9–12 | 5 |
| 13–16 | 6 |
| 17–20 | 7 |
| 21–24 | 8 |
| and so on | +1 per 4 weeks |

### Meal breaks and penalties

| Break | Window | Delayed | Curtailed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lunch | no later than **6h after Unit Call Time** | penalty at OT rate, 15 min increments | penalty at OT rate, 15 min increments, capped **1h on SWD / 30 min on SCWD** |
| Subsequent meal (30 min) | begins within **6h of end of previous break** | penalty at OT rate | penalty at OT rate |

- Worker may agree to forego the subsequent break *and* its penalty in favour of finishing earlier
- CWD: Producer provides a hot meal, 20 minutes to eat, no interruption of work, timing arranged within the department
- Work continuing more than 1 hour after the end of a CWD: Producer provides food and refreshments

### Turnaround and night work

- **Turnaround Period:** no less than **11 hours** between end of one work period and start of the next
- **Broken Turnaround:** comp rest (by end of that week or start of the next) **or** 0.5T enhancement. If comp rest is taken, **no payment is due**
- **Night Work:** scheduled shooting hours past **11pm**. Aggregated across the week, reconciled weekly, **capped at one working day**, then either comp rest or +1T
- Overtime after 11pm is **not** Night Work — it is 2T overtime
- 4+ consecutive weeks of night work: any full comp rest day due must be given as the **first day of the following working week**
- Contract ending on a night pattern: automatic **paid rest day**, max one per working week
- Comp rest must be given on a **working day**, not a designated rest day

### Clawback

| Day type | Max clawback per day |
|---|---|
| SWD | 1 hour |
| SCWD or CWD | 2 hours |

Unless mutually agreed with Workers. Producers will avoid unnecessary use and must account for commute, rush hour, unit moves and extended days.

### Travel — quick view

| Scenario | Threshold | Compensation |
|---|---|---|
| Home to Production Base | — | **Nothing payable**, time or cost |
| 30 Mile Radius option | beyond 30 road miles from Production Base | OT rate, 15 min increments, or forms part of the working day. Producer covers **cost** beyond 25 road miles |
| Within The M25 option | beyond the M25 | OT rate, 15 min increments, or forms part of the working day. Producer covers cost beyond that point |
| Via Unit Base to location | beyond 15 min each way | deducted from the working day, or OT rate if not deducted |
| Resident Location | beyond 20 road miles or 30 min from accommodation | OT rate, 15 min increments, or forms part of the working day |

One of the two Production Base options **must be elected on the Deal Memo** for the duration of the engagement. Mileage per HMRC guidelines. Overseas: base of operations is deemed Production Base and 30 Mile Radius is deemed selected.

### Bank holidays and holiday

- Working a UK Bank Holiday: **2T**
- Band 4, contracted but not working the Bank Holiday: paid at **BDR**
- Band 4 Workers **cannot** be required to take paid leave on UK Bank Holidays
- Holiday entitlement: **5.6 weeks** paid leave per annum inclusive of bank holidays, pro rata to contract length
- Daily contracts: holiday always takes the form of **payment in lieu**

### Budget bands

| Band | From 1 Jan 2023 | From 1 Jan 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | up to £850,000 | up to £1,250,000 |
| 2 | £850,000–£3,000,000 | £1,250,000–£3,000,000 |
| 3 | £3,000,000–£7,000,000 | £3,000,000–£8,000,000 |
| 4 | above £7,000,000 | above £8,000,000 |

Based on budgeted cost of one broadcast hour, excluding breakage. Notified in the Deal Memo.

### Cancelling dailies

| Notice given | Payment |
|---|---|
| By **1pm** the working day preceding the call | Nothing payable |
| After 1pm | **Full applicable Daily Rate** |

Applies only to dailies **confirmed** rather than pencilled. Not confirmed by 1pm the day before → deemed not hired, free to accept other work. Notice may be by any recorded means including text, email and WhatsApp.

## Differences At A Glance

The traps if you work across all three UK agreements:

| | **Scripted TV** | **Film (MMP)** | **APA (commercials)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold to apply | All scripted TV, **four budget bands** | Budget **≥ £30m** | All commercials |
| Shooting SWD | **10 worked + 1 lunch** = 11h | **11 worked + 1 lunch** = 12h | 10 worked + 1 lunch = 11h |
| CWD | **9 hours**, 20 min rest | **10 hours**, no break | 9 hours |
| SCWD | **9.5 hours + 30 min** | **10 hours + 30 min** | — |
| Hourly Rate | **BDR ÷ 10** | weekly ÷ 55, daily ÷ 11 | BDR ÷ 10 |
| Camera OT | **1.5T** | **2T** | Graded 1.5 / 1.25 / 1.0 |
| OT increments | **15 min, rounded up** | Camera 15 min, non-camera 30 min | Rounded up to 30 min |
| OT floor / ceiling | **£35 min / £70 max** | £25 min (camera) / £81.82 max | None |
| Night work | **After 11pm**, aggregated weekly, comp rest or +1T | After midnight, £20 premium + turnaround day | Call-time band, flat 2 × BHR |
| Early start | **Early Call before 06:00 → 1.5T** | Pre-Dawn Call before 05:00 → 2T | Early Call 05:00–07:00 → OT rate |
| Turnaround | **Broken Turnaround**, 11h, comp rest **or** 0.5T | 1.5T, capped £45/hr | TOC, paid on top of BDR |
| Delayed lunch | **Overtime Rate** | Standard Hourly Rate (1T) | £10 flat |
| Curtailed lunch | **Overtime Rate**, capped 1h SWD / 30m SCWD | Camera OT rate (2T) | OT starts earlier |
| 6th day | **1.5T shooting** | 1.5T, min 8h shooting | — |
| 7th day | **2T** | 2T, min 8h shooting | — |
| 8th day | **Strictly prohibited** | Not addressed | — |
| Cancel dailies | **1pm the day before** | 3pm days / 3am nights | §7 sliding scale |
| Extra premiums | **Split day £30, 11-day fortnight £100** | Night work £20 | — |

**The three traps if you work across film and TV:**

1. **Hourly Rate divisor.** TV is BDR ÷ 10. Film is weekly ÷ 55 or daily ÷ 11. The same day rate produces a different hourly rate.
2. **Camera OT is 1.5T on TV, 2T on film.** And film's third camera OT hour stops pro-rating; TV rounds up to 15 minutes throughout.
3. **Delayed lunch pays at the Overtime Rate on TV, at 1T on film.** Easy to over-claim or under-claim by crossing over.

# A. Applicability

## A.1 — Collective Agreement

**1.1** Pact recognises Bectu and Bectu recognises Pact as the sole representative organisations of workers ("Workers") and independent television producers ("Producers") for collective bargaining within this Agreement.

**1.2** This Agreement is the entire agreement between the parties. It supersedes and extinguishes all previous drafts, agreements (including the 2017 agreement), guidance, arrangements and understandings — including recommended terms published by or for Pact or by individual Bectu departments or branches, written or oral.

**1.3** Both parties pledge best endeavours to see the spirit of the Agreement honoured by their members, and pledge not to endorse, publish, support or recommend any terms that vary or purport to vary it.

## A.2 — Scope

**2.1** Applies to all scripted television produced in the UK by any UK independent production company, or any non-UK production company engaging UK-based crew in the UK ("Qualifying Productions").

**2.2** "Television" includes all forms whether now existing or hereafter invented, including made-for-TV features, subscription television, SVOD and other video on demand.

**2.3 Excluded from scope:**
- Multi-camera productions (studio and outside broadcast)
- Features
- Continuing drama / soaps
- Factual, including factual incorporating dramatic reconstruction
- Documentary
- Light entertainment

**2.4** Location filming outside the UK forming part of an in-scope production is governed by this Agreement unless expressly stated otherwise.

## A.3 — Budget Bands

**3.1** Each Qualifying Production falls into one of four bands, based on the budgeted cost of one broadcast hour excluding breakage:

| Band | Budget per broadcast hour |
|---|---|
| 1 | up to £850,000 |
| 2 | £850,000–£3,000,000 |
| 3 | £3,000,000–£7,000,000 |
| 4 | above £7,000,000 |

**3.2** From 1 January 2024 the bands are revised to:

| Band | Budget per broadcast hour |
|---|---|
| 1 | up to £1,250,000 |
| 2 | £1,250,000–£3,000,000 |
| 3 | £3,000,000–£8,000,000 |
| 4 | above £8,000,000 |

**3.3** The Producer notifies Workers of the applicable Band in the Deal Memo.

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — §3:**
> Budget band data will be shared with Bectu by Pact on specific request when Pact is first informed by production, as well as being included in deal memos.
>
> **On §3.1 / §3.2 transition:** productions that commenced principal photography **before January 2024** remain in the 2023 band structure (§3.1). Productions starting principal photography **on or after 1 January 2024** come under the new bands (§3.2).

## A.4 — Implementation And Review

**4.1** Applies to all Qualifying Productions, existing and new, from **1 January 2023** ("Implementation Date"), subject to one transition: a production that commenced principal photography in 2022 and completed more than 50% of its filming schedule in 2022 need not implement the Agreement for any Worker in any department, provided it is scheduled to complete principal photography by **31 March 2023**.

**4.2** Pact and Bectu officials meet every six months from the Implementation Date to review how the Agreement is working and jointly agree any clarifications of interpretation. Neither party may unilaterally publish guidance.

**4.3** Remains in full force for a minimum of 3 years, i.e. until **1 January 2026**, after which either party may give six months' written notice to terminate.

# B. Core Terms Of Engagement

## B.1 — The Working Day

### 1.1 Contracted Hours

Each Worker is issued a **Deal Memo** specifying the main conditions of engagement and setting out the standard **Contracted Hours** required before Overtime is due.

### 1.2 The Shooting Day

The shooting day is **paid as 10 Contracted Hours** and may be scheduled in three ways, or any combination:

| Day type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard Working Day (SWD)** | 10 shooting hours with 1 hour unpaid lunch for all on-set crew | 8am–7pm |
| **Semi-Continuous Working Day (SCWD)** | 9.5 shooting hours with 30 minute unpaid lunch | 8am–6pm |
| **Continuous Working Day (CWD)** | 9 shooting hours without a formal break or general cessation of work, but allowing a rest break of no less than 20 minutes for all on-set crew, timed by Workers within their department | 8am–5pm |

### 1.3 Contracted Work Before And After Shooting Day

Workers in the following departments are engaged to provide, and are paid for, **10 + 1 + 1 Contracted Hours** during the shooting period:

- Assistant Directors
- Costume
- Hair and Make-up (and Prosthetics)
- Locations
- Production
- Script Supervisors

### 1.4 Daily Rate, Hourly Rate And Additional Contracted Hour

**(a)** **Basic Daily Rate** = the contractual rate payable for a 10 hour day. **Hourly Rate** = BDR ÷ 10. **Daily Rate** = the 10+1 or 10+1+1 daily rate as applicable.

**(b)** Workers in the §1.3 departments are contracted for 11 Contracted Hours during the shooting period. The eleventh hour ("**Additional Contracted Hour**", ACH) is paid at the Hourly Rate. If BDR is £250, the ACH is £25.

**(c)** The Deal Memo specifies this as:

```
Shooting Period:
Total Contracted Hours                    11
10 shooting hours (Basic Daily Rate)      £250
Additional Contracted Hour (at Hourly Rate) £25
Total Daily Rate                          £275
```

**(d)** Workers on the ACH may be contracted on any of the three shooting days:

| Day type | With ACH |
|---|---|
| SWD | 10 shooting hours + ACH |
| SCWD | 9.5 shooting hours + ACH |
| CWD | 9 shooting hours + ACH |

**(e)** Payment for the ACH is **guaranteed** even where the Worker works less than their Contracted Hours.

**(f)** The ACH **cannot be aggregated across the working week**.

**(g)** The ACH **does not constitute Overtime** and forms part of the standard working day during the shoot period.

### 1.5 The Standard Day For Non-Shooting Crew

**(a)** The standard day for non-shooting crew is a **SWD** (10 + 1 hours with an unpaid lunch hour).

**(b)** Work done on non-shooting days, and work done by non-shooting crew, is **always contracted on a SWD basis**. Specified in the Deal Memo.

**(c)** A Head of Department may request that specified individuals in their department are temporarily scheduled on a non-shooting SCWD or CWD. Only where that individual's workload is not expected to exceed Contracted Hours, and **in exceptional circumstances only**. Request may be made **only by the HoD** and requires prior written approval from the Line Producer or Producer's Nominated Representative.

**(d)** Overtime on a non-shooting SWD or SCWD should only be incurred in **very exceptional circumstances** and only with prior written approval from the Producer's Nominated Representative.

**(e)** Where Overtime is incurred on a non-shooting SCWD, the Producer may elect to revert the pattern to a SWD with immediate effect from the following day's schedule.

**(f)** **Non-shooting CWDs do not incur Overtime.**

### 1.6 Ready For Work And Packing Up

**(a)** For all departments other than those in §1.3, the BDR is deemed to include an amount of time for prep and wrap customary to that department, to ensure the Worker is ready to start at their Individual Call Time.

**(b)** No specific time is allocated to this in the Deal Memo.

### 1.7 Call Times

**(a)** In pre-production, Producers and HoDs discuss shoot requirements, schedule, department size, where dailies may be needed, the **Unit Call Time** for each shooting day, and so far as possible each Worker's **Individual Call Time**.

**(b)** During each day of the shooting period, following discussions between the Producer's Nominated Representative and the relevant HoD, each Worker is given a call sheet specifying the next day's Unit Call Time. This is not expected to vary much, if at all, from the Unit Call Time proposed in pre-production or when the shooting schedule was issued.

**(c)** For all departments, the Individual Call Time is either the Unit Call Time, or if different, the call time specified on the call sheet.

**(d)** Any Individual Call Time varying from the Unit Call Time will have been discussed with the Worker's HoD and **must be pre-approved in writing by the Line Producer**.

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — call times and individual call times:**
> Individual call times have **replaced 'pre-calls'**. Work done prior to unit call is specified on the call sheet as that individual's call time. The expression 'pre-call' may still be used informally, but it now simply means starting before the unit call time. **This work is part of the individual's contracted hours.**
>
> Overtime only applies once those contracted hours have been worked. This means there will frequently be situations where **some crew are in overtime and some are still working their contracted hours** — either because they work the ACH, or because they started at a different time.
>
> **Example 1**
> Unit call (SWD): 08.00.
> Crew Member A is contracted 10+1+1, individual call 07.30.
> Crew Member B is contracted 10+1, individual call 08.00.
> At 19.00 thirty minutes camera overtime is called.
> - 19.00–19.30: **Crew Member A earns 1T** — still working their daily contracted hours.
> - 19.00–19.30: **Crew Member B earns 1.5T** — has exceeded their daily contracted hours.
> - If both continued past 19.30, **both would earn 1.5T**, as A would then have exceeded their contracted hours.
>
> **Example 2**
> Unit call (SWD): 08.00. Both A and B contracted 10+1.
> A has individual call 07.30. B has individual call 08.00.
> Filming wraps thirty minutes early at 18.30.
> **Both receive their full standard day rate. Neither has exceeded contracted hours. No overtime is due to either.**

### 1.8 Split Days

**(a)** A **Split Day** is a filming day with a camera finish between **9pm and 11pm**.

**(b)** Producers may schedule Split Days as they elect, subject to a cap before a premium is due:

| Worker engagement (including dailies) | Cap |
|---|---|
| 0–7 weeks of a shooting schedule | **No cap** |
| Over 7 weeks of a shooting schedule | **40%** of total filming days in the Worker's overall shooting schedule |

**(c)** A **£30 premium** is paid for each Split Day worked in excess of the cap. A Split Day is worked if the Worker works **any part** of the shooting hours 9pm–11pm.

**(d)** Split days are reconciled at the end of the contract and any premium due is paid then.

### 1.9 Early Call

**(a)** Excluding any time constituting Night Work under §5, time worked at the Producer's request or prior approval **before 06.00** ("Early Call") is paid at an enhanced rate. The enhancement is **0.5T**, so time worked before 06.00 (**rounded up to the nearest 15 minutes**) is paid at **1.5T**.

**(b)** Early Call **does not constitute Night Work** and attracts no additional entitlement under §5.

**(c)** Early Call **is not Overtime and is not capped at the Maximum Overtime Rate**.

## B.2 — The Working Week

### 2.1 Week Schedules

Producers work predominantly on 5 day weeks, typically Monday to Friday, but may nominate any day as a working day due to production exigencies, or engage Workers for fewer than 5 days a week (for example to enable part time work and job sharing). Subject to any standard or pre-agreed hiatus or stand-down called on reasonable notice, Workers are contracted to work **on average 10 days per fortnight**.

### 2.2 Weekend Working

**(a)** Weekend working must be scheduled on **minimum 2 weeks' notice** for Workers to be contractually obliged to work it.

**(b)** With less than 2 weeks' notice the Worker has **no obligation** to work that weekend. Workers electing not to work take the day(s) as **unpaid rest day(s)**.

**(c)** Workers given minimum 2 weeks' notice are required to work but may request leave under §11.5.

**(d)** Weekend working is paid at the Worker's Daily Rate **unless** the sixth or seventh consecutive day provisions apply.

### 2.3 Sixth Consecutive Days

**(a)** A sixth consecutive **shooting** day is paid at **1.5T**.

**(b)** A sixth consecutive **non-shooting** day, worked at the Producer's request or with prior written approval of the Producer's Nominated Representative:

| Hours worked | Payment |
|---|---|
| Up to 6 hours | 10 hour BDR at **1T** |
| More than 6 hours | BDR at **1.5T** |

**If prior written approval is not given, no payment is due.**

**(c)** If the sixth consecutive day is a UK bank holiday, the Daily Rate is **2T** in lieu of §2.3(a) and §2.3(b).

### 2.4 Seventh Consecutive Days

**(a)** A seventh consecutive day worked at the Producer's request or with prior written approval is paid at **2T**.

**(b)** Producers **will not schedule 7 consecutive filming days** for any one Worker.

### 2.5 Prohibition On More Than Seven Consecutive Days

**(a)** Eight consecutive days' work is **strictly prohibited**.

**(b)** In no circumstances should any Worker work more than seven consecutive days. Workers who have worked seven consecutive days **must take a rest day**. HoDs and Producers agree to ensure compliance for crew working on non-shooting days.

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — §2.3, §2.4, §2.5:**
> Crew should be supported by producers and HoDs to **avoid** working 6th and 7th consecutive days, through responsible scheduling and, where possible, the use of dailies.
>
> **No consecutive 8th days are permitted under any circumstance.**
>
> Non-shooting sixth days require **prior written approval** before they can be worked. This approval must be given by the producer's nominated representative, who will usually be the **line producer**.

## B.3 — Eleven Day Fortnights

**3.1** Producers may schedule 11 day fortnights during the shooting period as they elect, subject to a cap on sixth days scheduled as part of an 11 day fortnight:

| Number of shoot weeks | Cap |
|---|---|
| 0–8 weeks | 4 |
| 9–12 weeks | 5 |
| 13–16 weeks | 6 |
| 17–20 weeks | 7 |
| 21–24 weeks | 8 |
| and so on | |

**3.2** Where the Producer requires an additional sixth day as part of an 11 day fortnight **beyond the cap**, the Worker is entitled to a **£100 premium** per additional sixth filming day worked beyond the cap.

**3.3** The £100 premium is paid **in addition to** the applicable Daily Rate in the Deal Memo — and in the case of a sixth consecutive shooting day, that rate is uplifted per §2.3.

---

## B.4 — Clawback

**4.1** Clawback is the process of adjusting shoot hours each day to work backwards to an earlier call time for the unit.

**4.2** Producers should consider the effect on commute time (call time clashing with rush hour and so on), location and unit moves, extended days. Production will take local traffic and travel conditions into account when calling crew where clawback is used.

**4.3** Producers will **avoid the unnecessary use** of clawback.

**4.4** Clawback permitted:

| Day type | Maximum |
|---|---|
| SWD | 1 hour per day |
| CWD or SCWD | 2 hours per day |

Unless mutually agreed with Workers.

## B.5 — Night Work

**5.1** **Night Work is scheduled shooting hours worked past 11pm.** All Overtime after 11pm is paid at 2T per §7.10.

**5.2** All Night Work is **rounded up to the nearest 15 minutes**.

**5.3** Night Work is **aggregated across the week and reconciled weekly**. At the end of each week, the total Night Work time in that week (**capped at one working day**) is either:

- **(a)** given back as compensatory rest by the end of the week or on the first day of the following working week; **or**
- **(b)** paid at **1T in addition** to the rate otherwise due, meaning the Worker receives a total of **2T** for that time.

**5.4** Where there have been **four or more consecutive weeks of Night Work** and a full compensatory rest day is due at the end of the final week, that rest day **must be given as the first day of the following working week**.

**5.5** Where a Worker's contract **ends on a night working pattern**, that Worker is automatically entitled to a **paid rest day** in lieu of any §5.3 entitlement. Applies to all Workers **including dailies**, provided no Worker is entitled to more than one paid rest day under this clause per working week.

**5.6** A paid compensatory rest day **does not constitute a working day**.

**5.7** Where the Producer elects to give compensatory rest back, this **must be on a working day, not a designated rest day**.

## B.6 — Rest Periods And Broken Turnaround

**6.1** Producers shall comply with the Working Time Regulations in scheduling daily and weekly rest, and should give Workers **no less than 11 hours rest** between the end of one work period and the start of the next ("**Turnaround Period**").

**6.2** Where the Worker is required to work during the Turnaround Period ("**Broken Turnaround**"):

- **(a)** the Producer shall wherever possible allow an **equivalent period of compensatory rest**, taken by the end of that week or the beginning of the following week; **or**
- **(b)** time worked in breach (**rounded up to the nearest 15 minutes**) is paid at an enhanced rate of **0.5T**, meaning a total of **1.5T** for that time.

**6.3** Where compensatory rest is taken under §6.2(a), **no payment is due** for that occurrence.

**6.4** Each 14 day period should include **at least two scheduled rest days**. Producers should endeavour to give the Turnaround Period immediately prior to any scheduled 24 hour rest period. Rest days may be consecutive or separate.

**6.5** Where a Worker is given two consecutive days off, Producers should endeavour to give the Turnaround Period immediately prior to the scheduled 48 hour rest period.

## B.7 — Overtime

**7.1** Contracted Hours are deemed to **start from the Individual Call Time**. Where the Worker, at the Producer's request or with prior approval, exceeds their total daily Contracted Hours, the excess constitutes **Overtime**.

**7.2** Overtime is payable at the **Hourly Rate × 1.5T**, subject to §7.10 and always subject to the Minimum and Maximum Overtime Rates ("**Overtime Rate**").

**7.3** **Minimum Overtime Rate: £35 per hour**, pro-rated for partial hours.

**7.4** **Maximum Overtime Rate: £70 per hour**, or if greater, the Worker's Hourly Rate at 1T — pro-rated for partial hours.

**7.5** Overtime (shooting and non-shooting) accrues in **15 minute increments**.

**7.6** **Camera Overtime can only be called once** and must be called as a specified number of 15 minute increments. **No grace periods shall apply.**

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — §7.6:**
> If, for example, 30 minutes camera overtime is called but the production finishes within 15 minutes, **the full 30 minutes must be paid**.
>
> In addition, if the production continues beyond 30 minutes, **crew can refuse to work the extra time without any detriment**.

**7.7** The Overtime Rate is also applied to the following **penalties**:
- **(a)** excess travel per §8.3, §8.4 and §8.5
- **(b)** delayed or curtailed meal breaks per §10.2

**7.8** Broken Turnaround is compensated per §6.2.

**7.9** Each situation giving rise to a payment at the Overtime Rate **accrues separately and may not be aggregated across the working day**.

**7.10** **Overtime at 2T ("Enhanced Overtime Rate"):**
- **(a)** Overtime for work **after 11pm**, whether shooting or non-shooting. For clarity, Overtime after 11pm **does not constitute Night Work**
- **(b)** Overtime on a **UK Bank Holiday**
- **(c)** Overtime on a **seventh consecutive day**

In each case in lieu of the Overtime Rate, and **subject to the Minimum and Maximum Overtime Rates**.

**7.11** **Camera Overtime on a CWD:** Producers recognise camera Overtime should not occur as a matter of course on a CWD, and agree it **should not be scheduled**. If it does take place it should be unscheduled and is subject to a cap of **no more than 2 hours per working week**. Overtime beyond this cap must be **agreed locally**, including an appropriate rate of pay.

**7.12** Overtime on a **sixth consecutive day** (shooting or non-shooting) is paid at the standard **Overtime Rate**.

**7.13** The Minimum and Maximum Overtime Rates apply to **all** Overtime, **including Enhanced Overtime Rate**, and to **any penalties paid at Overtime Rate**.

**7.14** **Overtime does not arise where a Worker works fewer than their Contracted Hours.**

**7.15** Notwithstanding anything to the contrary, no time in excess of Contracted Hours shall be worked, nor services or travel undertaken, nor meal breaks delayed or curtailed, where any of these would give rise to payment — **unless specifically requested by the Producer or pre-approved by the Producer's Nominated Representative**.

## B.8 — Travel

### 8.1 Definitions

- **Production Base** — usually where the production office is located, and will be so unless otherwise designated in the Deal Memo. A Producer can nominate **one UK Production Base per Worker**.
- **Unit Base** — usually the location of catering, facilities vehicles and/or unit parking servicing that day's shooting.
- **Resident Location** — a place of work **over 50 road miles** from Production Base which a Worker cannot be expected to travel to and from each day.

### 8.2 Travel To Production Base

**No payments** for time spent or costs associated with travelling from home to and from Production Base.

### 8.3 Travel To Unit Base Or Filming Location

Producers **must elect on the Deal Memo** which one of the following applies for the duration of the engagement:

**(a) 30 Mile Radius**
- No payments for time travelling to and from any place **within 30 road miles** of Production Base
- Time travelling **beyond the 30 road mile point** is compensated at the **Overtime Rate** (15 minute increments) or forms part of the working day
- The Producer is responsible for the **cost** of travel beyond the point that is **25 road miles** from Production Base

**(b) Within The M25** (where Production Base is within the M25)
- No payments for time or costs travelling to and from any place **within the M25**
- Time travelling **beyond the M25** is compensated at the **Overtime Rate** (15 minute increments) or forms part of the working day, and the Producer is responsible for the cost beyond that point

> **Note on §8.3(a):** the time threshold is 30 road miles but the cost threshold is 25 road miles. This asymmetry is as printed in the agreement, not a transcription error.

### 8.4 Travel Via Unit Base

Travel **in excess of 15 minutes** to, and 15 minutes from, Unit Base to filming location is **included in the working day** where the Worker is required by the Producer to travel via Unit Base. Time is **deducted from the working day** to account for travel in excess of 15 minutes between Unit Base and filming location at the beginning and end of the day. **If time is not deducted, a payment at the Overtime Rate is due** for the excess travel time (15 minute increments).

### 8.5 Travel To Or From Resident Location

Where travel for work purposes is to a place **more than 20 road miles or 30 minutes** from the place at which overnight accommodation is provided, time spent travelling beyond that threshold is compensated at the **Overtime Rate** (15 minute increments) or forms part of the working day, and the Producer is responsible for the cost beyond that point.

### 8.6 Travel On Scheduled Rest Days

Where a Worker **elects** to return home on a scheduled rest day when working at a Resident Location or outside the UK, time and costs are **not paid**. Where the Worker is **asked** to travel for production purposes on a scheduled rest day, the Producer shall **agree a payment** to compensate for disrupting a rest day.

### 8.7 Mileage And Expenses

Mileage costs (for use of own vehicle, where approved in advance by the Producer) are reimbursed **in accordance with HMRC guidelines**. Mileage is **not** paid where the Producer provides reasonably accessible transport, where car allowance already forms part of the deal terms, or where the Worker is driving a hired vehicle.

### 8.8 Travel On Overseas Locations

The base of operations overseas is **deemed the Production Base** for the duration of the overseas filming, and the Producer is **deemed to have selected the 30 Mile Radius option** in §8.3 by reference to that base.

### 8.9 Travel Time

References to travel time in §8 assume **normal road traffic and weather conditions**.

### 8.10 Consecutive Days

**Travel days do not count** for the purpose of calculating sixth and seventh consecutive days.

### 8.11 Expenses — Resident And Overseas Locations

Reasonable out-of-pocket expenses (per diems) are **agreed by the Producer and referenced in the Deal Memo**.

## B.9 — Travel By Air

**9.1** It is the **Producer's responsibility** to make necessary bookings where the Worker is required to travel by air. All flights shall be on scheduled passenger services, or charter flights with safety standards conforming to those of the UK Civil Aviation Authority.

**9.2** **Time off after air travel:** Producers shall take into account time changes and duration of travel when scheduling work following air travel. For long-haul flights the Producer shall take into account the standard of seating and services afforded by that airline.

**9.3** **Excess baggage:** the Worker is responsible for the cost of transporting excess baggage. Where the excess is caused by carriage of **pre-approved tools of the trade**, or where agreed in advance, the cost is the **Producer's** responsibility.

## B.10 — Providing And Paying For Meals

**10.1 SWD and SCWD:** where the Worker works away from Production Base as part of the shooting crew, the Producer **provides lunch**. If not provided, the Producer provides an agreed payment in lieu, or reimburses the cost on production of a valid receipt (including VAT details where applicable). Subject to a reasonable maximum cost agreed in advance. Where no maximum has been agreed, payment is made with reference to **HMRC guidelines**, provided valid receipts for actual costs are supplied.

**10.2** During principal photography Workers are entitled to take lunch **no later than 6 hours after the Unit Call Time**. If asked by the Producer to:

- **(a) delay** their lunch break — penalty paid in **15 minute increments at the Overtime Rate** for the period of delay, pro-rated for partial hours
- **(b) curtail** their lunch break — penalty paid in **15 minute increments at the Overtime Rate** for the period curtailed, pro-rated, and **capped at 1 hour on a SWD and 30 minutes on a SCWD**

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — §10.2, breaks for crew that begin before unit call:**
> Crew starting before unit call are entitled to an **uninterrupted 20 minute break** in accordance with the Working Time Regulations **within 6 hours of their individual call time**.
>
> This is the responsibility of the HoD and/or their supervisor within each team, and should be organised so the filming schedule is not disrupted.

**10.3** For non-shooting crew the HoD should manage the department so Workers can take their lunch break (save where requested by the Producer as above). Where a HoD wishes to curtail or delay lunch, **prior written approval must be sought** from the Producer's Nominated Representative.

**10.4** A **subsequent meal break of half an hour** begins **within 6 hours of the end of the previous meal break**. A delay or curtailment requested by the Producer results in the payment set out above, **unless the Worker has agreed to forego the break and the penalty in favour of finishing earlier**. Where work continues **more than one hour after the end of a CWD**, the Producer shall provide food and refreshments.

**10.5** When a shooting **CWD** is scheduled, the Producer shall provide a **hot meal** to be taken without interruption of work. Each Worker shall have **20 minutes** to eat, without interruption of production, timed by Workers within their department. Adequate steps shall be taken by the Producer and HoD to ensure all Workers can access meals where catering is provided — which may require setting food and drink aside for consumption until the Worker's schedule allows.

## B.11 — Holiday Entitlement And Bank Holidays

**11.1** Workers directly engaged as workers are entitled to **5.6 weeks of paid leave** (pro-rata) per annum **inclusive of bank holidays**, pro rata to length of contract.

**11.2** Where required to work on a **UK Bank Holiday**, the Worker is entitled to be paid at **2T** — whether working on a weekly or longer term basis, or engaged as a daily.

**11.3** Where a Worker on a **Band 4** Qualifying Production is contracted during a period in which a UK Bank Holiday falls but is **not required to work** that day, the Worker is entitled to be paid at their **Basic Daily Rate** for that Bank Holiday.

**11.4** The Producer may nominate days (including UK Bank Holidays) on which Workers are required to take paid leave, and may make an appropriate deduction from accrued holiday entitlement. **Workers on Band 4 productions cannot be asked to take paid leave on UK Bank Holidays.**

> **Guidance (8 December 2023) — §11.4, UK Bank Holidays:**
> **All UK Bank Holidays must be observed**, including the holidays in **Scotland and Northern Ireland** if crew are working in either Scotland or NI.
>
> The **UK production base is the point of reference** for deciding which holidays are observed, **regardless of the location**.
>
> Reference: [UK bank holidays — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays)

**11.5** A Worker with sufficient entitlement may **request** paid leave on particular days, and the Producer will give sympathetic consideration — but it is the **Producer's prerogative** to determine if and when paid leave is taken during the contract term. Both parties must abide by statutory notice periods.

**11.6** If a Worker has **not taken** their full entitlement by the end of contract, the Producer makes a payment equivalent to the BDR for the number of days and fractions not taken.

**11.7** If a Worker has taken **more than** their full entitlement, they may be required to repay a sum equivalent to their BDR for the days and fractions taken in excess.

**11.8** Where a Worker on a **daily contract or series of daily contracts** is entitled under the Working Time Regulations to accrue pro rata holiday for each day worked, this **always takes the form of a payment in lieu**.

# C. General Principles

## C.1 — Engaging Crew

**1.1** Producers will provide Workers with a **Deal Memo** setting out the key terms of engagement, in accordance with legislation requiring employers to give employees and workers the main conditions of employment when they start work.

**1.2** The Producer shall issue a **Contract of Engagement** no more than **one quarter of the way through the engagement**, or **within six weeks** of the start — whichever is shorter. Delay beyond this only in exceptional circumstances. Financial terms shall be **no less favourable** than the Deal Memo and this Agreement.

**1.3** A Worker is deemed to have accepted the Deal Memo terms if they render services after it has been issued — **except** where they have provided the Producer's Nominated Representative with **written objections within 48 hours** of receiving them. In that case the Producer either informs the Worker of a counter-offer or informs them that no amendments will be made; if the Worker then continues to render services, they are deemed to have accepted the counter-offer (if any) or else the original terms. **A Worker is not deemed to have accepted terms if they have not received a Deal Memo.**

**1.4** The Contract of Engagement and the Producer's published internal company policies ("Company Policies") may address matters also provided for in this Agreement. **The Contract of Engagement and Company Policies take precedence**, provided this does not conflict with or undermine the terms provided for under this Agreement.

**1.5 Daily Engagements:**
- Producers may cancel the engagement of dailies **previously confirmed** (rather than merely pencilled) **without payment** only if the Producer or HoD gives notice **by 1pm the working day preceding** that daily's call
- Where daily hires are **not confirmed by 1pm the day before** commencement, the Worker is deemed **not hired** and can accept other work
- Notice to cancel may be by **any means of recorded communication** including text, email and WhatsApp
- **Cancellation after 1pm means the full applicable Daily Rate is payable**

**1.6 Engagements of one week or more:** may start on any day of the week, and may be terminated by **one week's notice** or without notice in accordance with the Contract of Engagement. Payment in lieu of notice shall be for **one week or the remainder of the contract, whichever is less**.

## C.2 — Payment

**2.1** Where hired for a specified number of days, the first pay week starts on the first day of engagement or such day as the Producer nominates. Subject to signature of a contract of engagement where issued:

- **(a) Schedule E / PAYE:** on weekly and daily engagements, the week's or day's **basic pay** is paid promptly, normally **one week in arrears**. Additional monies from overtime and other variables are paid **within 14 days** of the Producer receiving valid and undisputed written evidence of sums claimed. Where pay frequency varies from one week in arrears, the Producer shall promptly advise Workers.
- **(b) Schedule D / personal service company:** payment on the **next processed payroll** after receipt of a valid and undisputed invoice from the Worker or their personal service company.

## C.3 — Scheduling

**3.1** Workers should be informed in the Deal Memo of the provisional working schedule **before contracting**. HoDs should be informed of scheduled hours and any changes, and should liaise with Producers if additional team members are needed. Producers should seek to **restrict excessively long working days** and take careful account of hours needed for prep before, wrap after, and travel to and from Production Base and location.

**3.2** HoDs in consultation with Line Producers should consider **staggering Workers** where possible to manage day length, and Producers should **engage dailies** where necessary. Producers should give special consideration to offering **overnight accommodation or transport** where Workers are expected to work excessively long days.

**3.3** Producers shall endeavour to give the rest and other breaks set out in this Agreement. Breach may be unavoidable due to matters including access to facilities, talent, cast, weather or labour issues, or the need for continuity of service or production. In such cases Producers shall endeavour to ensure appropriate rest at the earliest opportunity, and that Broken Turnaround Periods are taken into account when scheduling (and paid at the applicable rate).

**3.4** HoDs and/or their department nominee should be informed of scheduled working hours and any changes, and should liaise with Producers if additional team members are needed. HoDs should endeavour to input during pre-production meetings on possible scheduling issues.

## C.4 — Basic Facilities

**4.1** Workers should have access to **running water and toilet facilities** at or within a short distance of any place used for work during periods of work. **Drinking water** should be available at every place of work.

## C.5 — Health And Safety

**5.1** Producers as employers have a **duty of care** to all Workers and must have in place a robust health and safety policy covering all stages of production — prep, filming and post — compliant with all relevant legislation and satisfying any commissioning broadcaster requirements. Producers agree to make their policies **available to Workers**.

## C.6 — Statutory Sick Pay

**6.1** The Producer will pay **SSP** where the engagement meets statutory qualifying criteria and the Worker satisfies statutory requirements.

## C.7 — Respect At Work

**7.1** Pact and Bectu are committed to a working environment in which everyone is treated with dignity and respect. **Bullying, harassment and victimisation are not tolerated**, and everyone working in the television industry is expected to behave respectfully and never act in a way that could be regarded as bullying, harassment and/or victimisation.

## C.8 — Insurance

**8.1** The Producer shall effect **Employer's Liability** insurance as required by law.

**8.2** The Producer shall effect adequate **Personal Accident, Health and Travel** insurance for all Workers required to work outside the UK.

**8.3** The Producer shall effect adequate insurance for **equipment hired from the Worker** — excluding tools of the trade which the Producer's insurer will not ordinarily insure — subject to the Worker providing an **inventory with serial numbers and replacement values**.

**8.4** In the event of the **death of a Worker** while on location in the UK or overseas, it is the Producer's responsibility to return the remains and personal effects to the last known UK residence of the deceased.

**8.5** When rendering services outside the UK, the Producer shall arrange insurance providing standard levels of cover for: personal accident and illness; medical and emergency travel expenses; and personal effects.

## C.9 — Unit Representatives

**9.1** Bectu unit representatives may be elected or appointed from time to time and will be given reasonable facilities to represent Workers' interests in connection with the work for which they are engaged. Subject to the Producer's prior consent, **not to be unreasonably withheld**, Bectu officials will be provided with reasonable facilities for meetings with members **during break periods**, complying with the Producer's reasonable directions. Such meetings shall not disrupt or interrupt work.

**9.2** Officials may be required to sign **confidentiality agreements** when seeking access to closed sets or other sensitive sites. Reasonable facilities will be offered for Union member Workers to meet in connection with their work. Such meetings normally take place **outside working hours** without interruption of work.

## C.10 — Dispute Procedure

**10.1** Pact and Bectu shall submit any differences or disputes arising solely in relation to the application of this Agreement in accordance with this procedure, **in a timely manner**, and agree to use this procedure **and no other** until exhausted.

**10.2** Where a dispute arises between a Worker and a Producer, either party — or Bectu or Pact — shall invoke the procedure **within seven days** of the cause arising.

**10.3** **First:** Workers should informally raise concerns with the HoD, Line Producer, Head of Production or Production Executive as referred to in the Deal Memo. Where informal efforts fail, the matter is referred to a senior representative of the Producer and the appropriate full time Bectu official, who shall make every endeavour to resolve it in consultation with the Worker and the Producer's representative.

**10.4** **Failing resolution** within a reasonable period, and in any event no later than **one week**, the matter is referred to an **independent mediator** jointly selected by Pact and Bectu, plus two representatives each of Pact and Bectu, who meet to discuss the issue within a reasonable period of a written request.

**10.5** The mediator hears from the Pact and Bectu representatives and makes a decision based on their submissions. **The mediator's decision is final and binding on both parties.**

**10.6** Producer and Worker agree to accept as final and binding any resolution reached under this procedure. **No stoppage of work or lock-out** shall take place until the procedure has been invoked and all stages exhausted. Whatever practice or custom was in operation before registration of the dispute shall continue until the procedures are exhausted.

Week 2 accrues 7 hours, given back as **7 hours**, not a whole day. Week 4 accrues exactly 10 hours — a full day — but this is the **final week of four or more consecutive weeks of Night Work**, so per §5.4 the compensatory rest day must be given as the **first day of the following working week**, hence Week 5 Monday.

### Example 2 — Worker works a block of four nights in one week (SWD)

| | **Week 1** |
|---|---|
| Mon | 2pm–1am |
| Tue | 1pm–12am |
| Wed | 1pm–12am |

| | **5 hours Night Work accrued** |

## Night Work And Overtime

### Example 3 — Worker works a block of five nights in one week (SWD)

| | **Week 1 — shooting schedule (SWD)** |
|---|---|
| Mon | 3pm–2am — 3 hours Night Work |
| Tue | 3pm–2am — 3 hours Night Work |
| Wed | 3pm–2am — 3 hours Night Work, followed by 1 hour de-rig finishing 3am — **1 hour overtime** |
| Thu | 3pm–2am — 3 hours Night Work, followed by 1 hour de-rig finishing 3am — **1 hour overtime** |
| Fri | 3pm–2am — 3 hours Night Work |
| | **15 hours Night Work accrued. 2 hours Overtime accrued** |

**Settlement:**
- **Night Work:** 10 hours, paid as a **paid compensatory rest day** (capped at one working day)
- **Overtime:** 2 hours at **2T**

> **What this illustrates (§7.10(a)).** The de-rig hours from 2am to 3am are **overtime, not Night Work** — Night Work is *scheduled shooting hours* past 11pm. Because that overtime falls after 11pm it pays at **2T**, and it does **not** feed into the weekly Night Work aggregation. The two payments run in parallel.

## Overtime

### Example 4 — Crew Member A, Contracted Hours 11

SWD worked during the shooting period, scheduled shooting 8am–7pm. Contracted Hours 11 means **10 shooting hours + 1 Additional Contracted Hour**, plus the 1 hour unpaid lunch — so a **12 hour elapsed clock** from individual call.

| Day | Work pattern | Overtime due |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 7am–7pm | Nil |
| Tue | 7am–8pm | 1 hour |
| Wed | 7am–6pm | Nil — worked **less** than Contracted Hours |
| Thu | 7am–8.10pm | 1 hour 10 mins above Contracted Hours = **1 hour 15 mins** |
| Fri | 9am–10.20pm | 1 hour 20 mins above Contracted Hours = **1 hour 30 mins** |

### Example 5 — Crew Member B, Contracted Hours 10

SWD worked during the shooting period, scheduled shooting 8am–7pm. Contracted Hours 10 means **10 shooting hours** plus the 1 hour unpaid lunch — an **11 hour elapsed clock** from individual call.

| Day | Work pattern | Overtime due |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 8am–7pm | Nil |
| Tue | 7.15am–7pm | 45 mins above Contracted Hours = **45 mins** |
| Wed | 7am–6pm | Nil — worked Contracted Hours |
| Thu | 6.40am–7pm | 1 hour 20 mins above Contracted Hours = **1 hour 30 mins** |
| Fri | 8am–7.25pm | 25 mins above Contracted Hours = **30 mins** |

> **What Examples 4 and 5 settle.**
>
> **The clock runs from the individual call time, not the unit call.** Example 5 Tuesday starts at 07.15 against an 8am unit call and bills 45 minutes of overtime at the end of an 11 hour day.
>
> **The overtime trigger is elapsed time, lunch included.** 10+1 = 11 hours elapsed. 10+1+1 = 12 hours elapsed. Example 4 Monday runs 7am–7pm, exactly 12 hours, and yields nil.
>
> **Overtime rounds UP to the nearest 15 minutes.** 1h10m → 1h15m. 1h20m → 1h30m. 25m → 30m. 45m stays 45m. §7.5's "accrues in 15 minute increments" means rounded up, not rounded to nearest.
>
> **A short day is not netted off.** Example 4 Wednesday and Example 5 Wednesday both yield nil rather than a negative balance, confirming §7.14.

## Sixth Consecutive Day (Non-Shooting)

### Example 6

Worker A works **9am–2pm**. Paid **10 hours at 1T**.

### Example 7

Worker B works **9am–6pm**. Paid **10 hours at 1.5T**.

> **What this illustrates (§2.3(b)).** Both workers are paid the full **10 hour** Basic Daily Rate regardless of the shorter hours actually worked. The only variable is the multiplier: **6 hours or less at 1T, more than 6 hours at 1.5T**. Worker A's 5 hours falls under the threshold; Worker B's 9 hours clears it.

## B. Core Terms

### 2.3, 2.4, 2.5 — Sixth, seventh and eighth consecutive day

Crew should be supported by producers and HoDs to avoid working 6th and 7th consecutive days, with responsible scheduling and, where possible, the promotion of dailies.

No consecutive 8th days are permitted under any circumstance.

Non-shooting sixth days require prior written approval before they can be worked. This approval must be given by the producer's nominated representative, who will usually be the line producer.

### 6 — Call time and individual call times *(maps to Core Terms §1.7)*

Individual call times have replaced 'pre-calls', so that work done prior to unit call will be specified in the call sheet setting out the individual's call time. The expression 'pre-call' may still be used informally, but it now simply means starting before the unit call time. **This work is part of an individual's contracted hours.**

Overtime will only apply once those contracted hours have been worked. This means there may frequently be situations where some crew are in overtime and some crew are still working their contracted hours — either because they work the ACH, or because they started work at a different time.

**Example 1**

Unit call (SWD): 08.00

Crew Member A is contracted on a 10+1+1 basis and has an individual call time of 07.30.
Crew Member B is contracted on a 10+1 basis and has an individual call time of 08.00.

At 19.00 thirty minutes camera overtime is called.

From 19.00–19.30 Crew Member A continues to earn **1T**, because they are still working their daily contracted hours.

From 19.00–19.30 Crew Member B earns **1.5T**, because they have exceeded their daily contracted hours.

If Crew Member A and Crew Member B both continued working after 19.30, both would earn **1.5T**, as Crew Member A would have exceeded their daily contracted hours.

**Example 2**

Unit call (SWD): 08.00

Crew Member A and Crew Member B are both contracted on a 10+1 basis.
Crew Member A has an individual call time of 07.30.
Crew Member B has an individual call time of 08.00.

Filming wraps thirty minutes early at 18.30.

Both Crew Member A and Crew Member B receive their full standard day rate. Neither has exceeded their contracted hours. **No overtime is due to either Crew Member.**

### 7.6 — Camera overtime

If, for example, 30 minutes camera overtime is called but the production finishes within 15 minutes, the full 30 minutes must be paid.

In addition, if the production continues beyond 30 minutes, crew can refuse to work the extra time without any detriment.

### 10.2 — Breaks for crew that begin before unit call

Crew starting before unit call are entitled to an uninterrupted 20 minute break in accordance with the Working Time Regulations, within 6 hours of their individual call time.

This is the responsibility of the HoD and/or their supervisor within each team, and should be organised so that the filming schedule is not disrupted.

### 11.4 — UK Bank Holidays

All UK Bank Holidays, including the holidays in Scotland and NI (if crew are working in either Scotland or NI), must be observed.

The UK production base should be the point of reference for deciding which holidays are observed, regardless of the location.

Reference: [UK bank holidays — GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays)

### Still open

**1. OT trigger on SCWD and CWD.** Every worked example in Section D uses an **SWD**. By the same elapsed-clock logic, SCWD should be 10 hours and CWD 9 hours from individual call, plus 1 hour each where the Additional Contracted Hour applies. This is a sound inference but it is still an inference — no example in the agreement demonstrates it.

**2. Maximum Overtime Rate above £70/hr.** §7.4 caps overtime at "£70 per hour or, if greater, the Worker's Hourly Rate at 1T". Above an HR of £70 (BDR above £700) the cap becomes HR × 1T rather than 1.5T — the multiplier effectively disappears at high day rates. Worth confirming against a real payslip.

**3. §8.3(a), 30 vs 25 road miles.** Time compensation begins beyond 30 road miles; cost responsibility begins beyond 25. As printed in the agreement.

**4. Early Call and the Max OT Rate.** §1.9(c) explicitly exempts Early Call from the Maximum Overtime Rate. Broken Turnaround (§6.2) and Night Work (§5.3) enhancements are silent. §7.13 applies the caps to "all Overtime" and "any penalties which are paid at Overtime Rate", and neither of these is expressed as an Overtime Rate payment — which suggests they are uncapped. Not stated explicitly.

**5. Night Work cap and the length of a "working day".** §5.3 caps weekly Night Work aggregation at "one working day". Example 1 treats 15 hours, 12 hours and 10 hours as all resolving to a single compensatory rest day, which implies a working day means **10 hours** here rather than 11 or 12. Consistent, but inferred from the examples rather than defined.
