# Pact/Bectu Agreement — Terms Applicable To Major Motion Pictures

**Effective:** as amended, 5 April 2021
**Applies to:** all crew (**except construction**) engaged on Major Motion Pictures commencing principal photography after Monday 5 April 2021
**Signatories:** Spencer MacDonald (National Secretary, Bectu Sector of Prospect), Max Rumney (Deputy CEO, Director of Business Affairs, Pact)
**Source:** `pact-bectu-agreed-terms-applicable-to-major-motion-pictures-as-amended-fe.pdf` (union.bectu.org.uk), doc ref 11186226-5 — this is a markdown rebuild of the same content for easier reference

> **Three separate rulebooks now.** This is the **feature film** agreement. See `PACT_BECTU_TV_RULES.md` for scripted TV and `APA_RULES.md` for commercials. The maths is different in all three — see [Differences At A Glance](#differences-at-a-glance).

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

### What qualifies

A **Major Motion Picture** is a feature film intended for initial cinematic exhibition, **or** a single piece of theatric-type content (not a series) intended for a global SVOD platform, with a production budget of **£30,000,000 or more** (the Major Picture Threshold).

If pre-production commences below the threshold, this Agreement does not apply. If the budget later rises to meet or exceed it for unforeseen reasons, the producer enters good faith discussions with Bectu.

### The week and the day

**Standard Working Week: 55 hours over 5 days.** May be varied by the producer on payment of overtime and premiums. **All overtime is voluntary.**

| Who / when | Day structure | Elapsed | OT after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-production, and non-shooting crew during principal photography — **SWD** | 11 hours worked + 1 hour lunch | 12h | 12h |
| Shooting crew — **SWD** | 11 hours worked + 1 hour lunch | 12h | 12h |
| Shooting crew — **CWD** | 10 working hours, no formal lunch break | 10h | 10h |
| Shooting crew — **SCWD** | 10 working hours + 30 min lunch | 10.5h | 10.5h |
| **Rigging Electricians** | 9 hours worked + 1 unpaid hour lunch | 10h | **10h elapsed** |

All three shooting-day variations are **paid as 11 worked hours**, at the producer's discretion. A SCWD will only run where needed by reason of location, the director's requirements, or exceptional circumstances.

Add the prep and wrap allowance below before overtime starts for the listed departments.

### Hourly Rate

| Crew type | Hourly Rate |
|---|---|
| Weekly | contractual weekly rate ÷ **55** |
| Daily | contractual daily rate ÷ **11** |
| Rigging Electricians (weekly) | contractual weekly rate ÷ **45** |
| Rigging Electricians (daily) | contractual daily rate ÷ **9** |

### Prep and wrap

| Departments | Allowance |
|---|---|
| ADs, Costume, Hair and Make-up, Locations, Script Supervisor, VFX — **excluding workshop crew rendering services in a workshop** | Rate deemed to include, **in addition to the 55 hours**, up to **30 minutes at the start and 30 minutes at the end** of each day, if required and per current working practices, with **no overtime due**. **Not aggregated across a day** |
| All other departments | Rate deemed to include an amount of prep and wrap customary to that department, to be ready to start at unit call |

### Overtime

| Type | Rate | Increments | Min | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Camera Overtime** (shooting crew, filming beyond the day) | **2T** | First 2 hours in **15 min** increments, pro-rated. **From the 3rd hour, not pro-rated for partial hours** | **£25/hr** (shooting crew) | **£81.82/hr** |
| **Non-Camera Overtime** (including pre-calls and de-rigs) | **1.5T** | **30 min** increments, pro-rated | — | **£81.82/hr** |

- **No Grace Period applies to Camera Overtime**
- Best practice to endeavour to avoid a **3rd hour** of camera overtime on any working day
- **All overtime must be expressly pre-approved** by the relevant HOD **and** the line producer/UPM
- The **Maximum Overtime Rate applies to all hourly rates uplifted under the agreement**, including 6th and 7th days, Bank Holidays and Pre-Dawn Calls
- Post Production crew get **Non-Camera Overtime only** — no Camera Overtime

**Grades over £3,000/week:** for the grades listed in [§3.3(d)](#33-overtime-rates), overtime eligibility where the weekly rate exceeds £3,000 per 55-hour week is **negotiated case by case**, subject always to the Maximum Overtime Rate.

### 6th and 7th days

| Day | Rate | Minimum guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| 6th consecutive day or night | **1.5T** for actual hours worked | 6 hours non-shooting, **8 hours shooting** |
| 7th consecutive day | **2T** for actual hours worked | 6 hours non-shooting, **8 hours shooting** |

- Overtime on a 6th day: per §3.3 (normal rates)
- Overtime on a 7th day: **also 2T**
- **Saturdays and Sundays are not premium days** unless they are consecutive 6th or 7th days worked
- All 6th/7th day hourly rates are subject to the Minimum Camera Overtime Rate and capped at the Maximum Overtime Rate
- **Travel days** under an official movement order do **not** reset the count, do **not** count towards it, and are **not** paid as a 6th or 7th day

### Premiums and penalties

| Item | Trigger | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Dawn Call** | Department or crew member called **before 05:00** | **2T until 05:00**, then normal Hourly Rate. Any call **from** 05:00 is a normal working day |
| **Night Work Premium** | Taking part in Night Work (including dailies) | **£20.00 per night actually worked** |
| **Turnaround Day** | End of a scheduled period of Night Work | **One day at standard contractual daily rate** |
| **Broken Turnaround** | Working inside the 11-hour turnaround | **1.5T**, 30 min increments, pro-rated, **capped £45/hr (£22.50 per 30 min)** |

**Pre-Dawn Call is not Night Work** and attracts no Night Work Premium.

### Night Work

**Definition:** shooting hours which continue **after midnight**, or **any unit call between midnight and 03:00**.

- If the camera is wrapped by midnight, wrap work after midnight appropriate to the position is **not** Night Work
- If a department is asked to work overnight for reasons **other than shooting**, that department still receives the Night Work Premium

**Night block scheduling:**

| Transition | Minimum rest |
|---|---|
| 5 night block → 5 night block | **48 + 11 hours** |
| 5 night block → 6 night block | **48 + 11 hours** |
| 6 night block → 6 night block | **24 + 11 hours** |
| 6 night block → 5 night block | **24 + 11 hours** |
| 6 consecutive nights → day work | **24 + 11 hours**, plus the turnaround day payment |
| 5 consecutive nights → day work | **48 hours**, plus the turnaround day payment |

Consecutive nights are usually scheduled in blocks of 5. **6-night blocks may only run twice in succession**, after which 5 and 6 night blocks must alternate. 5-night blocks may run in succession without limit.

### Meal breaks

| Situation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Shooting crew, lunch **delayed** | 15 min increments at the **standard Hourly Rate**, pro-rated |
| Shooting crew, lunch **curtailed** | 15 min increments at the **camera Overtime rate**, pro-rated, capped **1 hour on SWD / 30 min on SCWD** |
| Non-shooting crew, lunch shortened from one hour | Infringed time in **30 min increments at 1.5T** |
| Post Production crew | **Lunch break penalties do not apply** |

- Lunch generally no later than **6 hours after unit call** on SWD and SCWD
- **CWD:** food provided from **4.5 hours after unit call**, running for **two hours**
- **SCWD:** food from **4.5 hours after unit call**, running two hours; crew given a **30-minute break** during this
- Food is served during the **third hour of any Camera Overtime**
- **Rest break:** producers and HODs work together to provide an additional **20-minute break** where an individual works in excess of **12 hours** on a CWD or SCWD

> Note the split: **delayed** lunch pays at the standard Hourly Rate (1T), **curtailed** lunch pays at the camera Overtime rate (2T). That asymmetry is deliberate in the text.

### Travel

| Scenario | Threshold | Payment |
|---|---|---|
| Home to Base, or home to a Recognised Studio | — | **Nothing payable** |
| Production Base to Non-Resident or Resident Location | — | **Mileage at HMRC rate** unless transport provided |
| Travel between Recognised Studios in a single day | — | Mileage per §6.2(b) unless transport provided |
| To/from a filming location | First **30 road miles each way** included in the contractual rate | Beyond 30 road miles: agreed rate, **30 min increments**, pro-rated, **capped £45/hr (£22.50 per 30 min)** |
| Hotel to Resident/Overseas Location | Same **30 road miles each way** | Same as above |
| Off-set Prep Crews, including electrical rigging crews | — | Travel to/from Production Base and any location is **part of the normal working day, not compensated**. Pre-approved OT that is travel is capped £45/hr, 30 min increments |

- Travel time and distance shall be **stipulated in the Location Manager's official movement order**
- Travel time where payable is paid at the **contractual daily/hourly rate with no uplifts or premiums**, even on a 6th day, 7th day or Bank Holiday
- Travel on a day outside the contracted working week: **contractual flat daily rate**, regardless of time zones or day of travel
- Rest days at a Resident or Overseas Location are **not paid unless actually worked** (pre-approved, at the appropriate rate)
- Travelling home on a rest day: **no payment** unless the producer instructed it

**Recognised Studios:** West London, Shepperton, Pinewood, Leavesden, Elstree, Ealing, Twickenham, Gillette, Longcross, 3 Mills. The list is regularly updated as new studios are established.

**Location definitions:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Non-Resident Location** | A place of work where an individual can be expected to travel to and from the Production Base each day |
| **Resident Location** | A place of work **more than 50 road miles** from the Production Base |
| **Overseas Location** | A place of work outside the United Kingdom |

### Bank holidays

| Situation | Payment |
|---|---|
| Contracted over a period including a Bank Holiday, **not required to work** | **Daily fee** for that Bank Holiday |
| **Required to work** a Bank Holiday | **2T**, whether weekly, longer term, or engaged as a daily |

"Bank Holiday" means a UK official public holiday, or a foreign official public holiday where work takes place abroad.

### Cancelling dailies

| Working | Cancel by | After that |
|---|---|---|
| **Days** | **3pm** the working day preceding the call | **Full daily rate payable** |
| **Nights** | **3am** the working night preceding the call | **Full daily rate payable** |

Applies only to dailies **confirmed** rather than merely pencilled. Notice by any recorded communication — text, email, WhatsApp. Crew other than dailies should refer to their contract.

**Box rental for dailies:** where box rental is required for the role, a **pro rata** fee shall be agreed, equivalent pro-rata to the weekly box rental fee that would be payable on a weekly engagement.

## Differences At A Glance

| | **Film (MMP)** | **Scripted TV** | **APA (commercials)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold to apply | Budget **≥ £30m** | All scripted TV, four budget bands | All commercials |
| Contracted week | **55 hours / 5 days** | ~10 days per fortnight | — |
| Shooting SWD | **11 worked + 1 lunch** = 12h | **10 worked + 1 lunch** = 11h | 10 worked + 1 lunch = 11h |
| CWD | **10 hours**, no break | **9 hours**, 20 min rest | 9 hours |
| SCWD | **10 hours + 30 min** | **9.5 hours + 30 min** | — |
| Hourly Rate | weekly ÷ 55, daily ÷ 11 | BDR ÷ 10 | BDR ÷ 10 |
| Camera OT | **2T** | 1.5T | Graded 1.5 / 1.25 / 1.0 |
| Non-camera OT | **1.5T** | 1.5T | Same as camera |
| OT increments | **Camera 15 min, non-camera 30 min** | 15 min, rounded up | Rounded up to 30 min |
| OT floor / ceiling | **£25 min (camera) / £81.82 max** | £35 min / £70 max | No floor or ceiling |
| Night work | **After midnight**, £20 premium + turnaround day | **After 11pm**, weekly aggregation, comp rest or +1T | Call-time band, flat 2 × BHR |
| Early start | **Pre-Dawn Call before 05:00 → 2T** | Early Call before 06:00 → 1.5T | Early Call 05:00–07:00 → OT rate |
| Broken turnaround | 1.5T, **capped £45/hr** | 1.5T (0.5T uplift), or comp rest | TOC, paid on top of BDR |
| Delayed lunch | **Standard Hourly Rate (1T)** | **Overtime Rate** | £10 flat |
| Curtailed lunch | **Camera OT rate (2T)** | Overtime Rate | OT starts earlier |
| 6th day | 1.5T, **min 8h shooting** | 1.5T shooting | — |
| 7th day | 2T, min 8h shooting | 2T | — |
| 8th day | Not addressed | **Strictly prohibited** | — |
| Cancel dailies | **3pm** (days) / **3am** (nights) | **1pm** | §7 sliding scale |
| Travel cap | **£45/hr** | Overtime Rate | Single time |

**The three traps if you work across film and TV:**

1. **Hourly Rate divisor.** Film is weekly ÷ 55 or daily ÷ 11. TV is BDR ÷ 10. Same day rate produces a different hourly rate.
2. **Camera OT is 2T on film, 1.5T on TV.** And film's third camera OT hour stops pro-rating — a partial hour bills as a full one.
3. **Delayed lunch pays at 1T on film.** On TV it pays at the Overtime Rate. Easy money to leave behind, or to over-claim.

## 1 — Applicability and Implementation

**1.1** Pact recognises Bectu and Bectu recognises Pact for collective bargaining as the sole representative organisations of crew members and producers within the application of this Agreement.

**1.2** Applies to **all crew members, with the exception of construction**, engaged on Major Motion Pictures that commence principal photography **after Monday 5 April 2021**.

**1.3** A **Major Motion Picture** means a feature film intended for initial cinematic exhibition, or a single piece of theatric-type content (i.e. **not a series**) intended for a global SVOD platform, with a production budget **equal to or in excess of £30,000,000** (the **Major Picture Threshold**).

**1.4** If a feature film commences pre-production with a budget **below** the Major Picture Threshold, this Agreement **does not apply**. If during pre-production or principal photography the budget is subsequently increased **for unforeseen reasons** to meet or exceed the threshold, the producer will enter into **good faith discussions with Bectu** to discuss a solution.

**1.5** This Agreement is the entire agreement between the parties and supersedes and extinguishes all previous drafts, agreements, arrangements and understandings — including recommended terms published by or on behalf of Pact or individual Bectu departments or branches, written or oral.

**1.6** Following implementation, the parties monitor implementation and liaise with each other and with members of the **Production Guild**. Implementation is reviewed at regular intervals, and for the first time **one year after implementation**.

## 2 — The Working Day and the Working Week

### 2.1 The Standard Working Week

The Standard Working Week is a **55-hour, 5-day work week**. This can be varied by the producer upon payment of overtime and other premiums as set out in this Agreement, **with the understanding that all overtime is voluntary**.

### 2.2 The Working Day — All crew during pre-production, and non-shooting crew during principal photography

Outside the period of principal photography/reshoots, and/or for non-shooting crew members, the standard working day comprises **11 hours worked plus 1 hour for lunch** (the **Standard Working Day**, SWD).

Qualified as follows:

**(a) Rigging Electricians** have a separate definition of Standard Working Day: **9 hours worked and 1 unpaid hour for lunch, with overtime due after 10 elapsed hours**. All overtime is based on the same principles as the shooting crew, but the Hourly Rate for the purposes of §3.3 is the **contractual weekly rate ÷ 45**, or as relevant the **contractual daily rate ÷ 9**.

**(b) Post Production Crew** follow the Standard Working Week and the Standard Working Day applicable to non-shooting crew, and are entitled to **Non-Camera Overtime** under §3.3(a)(ii) if applicable, **but not Camera Overtime**. Start times are when each crew member starts work, which will not necessarily be the same as Unit Call times for the Post Production Department. Lunch breaks may be **staggered** under the direction of the 1st Assistant Editor or Post Production Supervisor in co-ordination with production, and post-production crew are expected to manage their own time in order to take their breaks. **Lunch break penalties shall not apply.**

### 2.3 The Working Day — Shooting Crew

For shooting crew, the working day may comprise any of the following at the producer's discretion. **Each of the three variations is paid as 11 worked hours:**

| | Structure |
|---|---|
| **(a) Standard Working Day (SWD)** | The shooting day comprises **11 hours worked plus 1 hour for lunch** |
| **(b) Continuous Working Day (CWD)** | The shooting day runs for **10 working hours without a formal break for lunch** |
| **(c) Semi-Continuous Working Day (SCWD)** | The shooting day runs for **10 working hours plus a 30-minute break for lunch**. It is acknowledged that a SCWD will only run where needed by reason of the location, the director's requirements, or exceptional circumstances |

## 3 — Working Outside the Normal Working Day — Overtime

### 3.1 General

If a **non-shooting** crew member works hours in excess of the Standard Working Day, or if a **shooting** crew member works hours in excess of a SWD, CWD or SCWD as applicable, then overtime is payable **after expiry of the relevant Prep and Wrap** as defined in §3.2.

### 3.2 Prep and Wrap

**(a) Specific Departments.** For the following departments — **with the exception of workshop crew rendering services in a workshop** — the crew member's rate is deemed to include, **in addition to the 55 hours worked**, up to **30 minutes at the beginning and 30 minutes at the end of each day**, if required and in accordance with current working practices, **without any overtime being due**:

- ADs
- Costume
- Hair and Make-up
- Locations
- Script Supervisor
- VFX

**Prep and wrap will not be aggregated across a day.**

**(b) All other Departments.** The crew member's rate is deemed to include an amount of time for prep and wrap **customary to that department**, to ensure the crew member is ready to start the working day at unit call.

### 3.3 Overtime Rates

**(a)** For calculating overtime, the crew member's hourly rate is deemed to be their **contractual weekly rate ÷ 55** (**Hourly Rate**), or for daily crew members their **contractual daily rate ÷ 11** — save for Rigging Electricians, whose rates are per §2.2(a).

Overtime is calculated at either the Hourly Rate × **1.5 (1.5T)** or the Hourly Rate × **2 (2T)**, depending on whether the overtime is Camera Overtime or Non-Camera Overtime.

**(i) Camera Overtime.** Where the producer requests a **shooting** crew member to render services **for the purpose of filming** in excess of the shooting hours for a SWD, CWD or SCWD, the crew member is entitled to overtime at **2T**, pro-rated as set out below for partial hours, subject always to the Minimum Overtime Rate (§3.3(b)) and the Maximum Overtime Rate (§3.3(c)).

- **No Grace Period is applicable to Camera Overtime**
- The **first 2 hours** of Camera Overtime are paid in **15-minute increments** and pro-rated accordingly
- **From the 3rd hour, overtime will not be pro-rated for partial hours**
- It is **best practice to endeavour to avoid a 3rd hour** of camera overtime on any working day

**(ii) Non-Camera Overtime**, including **pre-calls and de-rigs**, is paid at **1.5T** for any overtime, payable in **30 minute increments** and pro-rated accordingly for partial hours, subject always to the Maximum Overtime Rate and the prep and wrap provisions of §3.2.

**(b) Minimum Camera Overtime Rate.** For **shooting** crew members, the Camera Overtime rate may be **no less than £25 per hour**.

**(c) Maximum Overtime Rate.** For **all** crew members, the overtime rate may be **no more than £81.82 per hour**. For the avoidance of doubt, the Maximum Overtime Rate applies to **all hourly rates uplifted** under this agreement, including in relation to **6th and 7th days, Bank Holidays and Pre-Dawn Calls**.

**(d) Grades over £3,000 per week.** For the following grades, eligibility for overtime payments where the crew member's weekly rate **exceeds £3,000 per 55 hour working week** will be **negotiated on a case-by-case basis**, and shall be subject always to the Maximum Overtime Rate:

- 1st AD
- Action Vehicle Co-Ordinator
- Assistant SFX Supervisor
- Costume Designer
- DOP
- Editor
- Financial Controller / Accountant
- Chief of Department for Make-Up and Hair Design
- Producer
- Production Designer
- Production Manager / UPM
- Production Supervisor
- Set Decorator
- SFX Supervisor
- Supervising Art Director
- VFX Supervisor
- VFX Producer
- Specialist Workshop Supervisor

**(e)** **All overtime must be expressly pre-approved by the relevant HOD and the line producer/UPM.**

## 4 — Working Outside the Normal Working Week — 6th and 7th Days

### 4.1 6th Days

**(a)** Any **6th consecutive day or night** worked is paid at **1.5T for actual hours worked**, with a minimum guarantee of **6 hours for non-shooting crew and 8 hours for shooting crew**.

**(b)** Any overtime worked on a 6th day is paid in accordance with §3.3.

### 4.2 7th Days

**(a)** Any **7th consecutive day** worked is paid at **2T for actual hours worked**, with a minimum guarantee of **6 hours for non-shooting crew and 8 hours for shooting crew**.

**(b)** Any overtime worked on a 7th consecutive day is **also paid at 2T** based on the individual hourly rate and in accordance with §3.3.

### 4.3 For the avoidance of doubt

**(a)** Saturdays and Sundays **shall not be paid as a premium day** unless they are consecutive 6th or 7th days worked.

**(b)** All hourly rates for 6th and 7th days are subject to the Minimum Camera Overtime Rate and **capped at the Maximum Overtime Rate**.

### 4.4 Treatment of Travel Days for the purposes of calculating 6th and 7th Day payments

For the purpose of calculating consecutive days or nights worked under §4, a day or night spent in travel **under an official movement order** shall **not**:

- **(a)** re-set the count of consecutive days or nights worked; nor
- **(b)** count towards the calculation of consecutive days or nights worked; nor
- **(c)** be paid as a consecutive 6th or 7th day or night worked.

**Worked example.** In the scenario below, the second Sunday is paid as a **6th consecutive day worked** under §4.1, while all remaining work days — and, per §6.2(h), the Saturday travel day — are paid at **1T**:

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest Day | | | | | | Rest Day |
| Work Day 1 | Work Day 2 | Work Day 3 | Work Day 4 | Work Day 5 | **Travel Day** | **Work Day 6** |
| Rest Day | | | | | | Rest Day |

## 5 — Other Terms, Premiums and Penalties

### 5.1 Pre-Dawn Calls

For any department or crew member called **before 05:00**, overtime is paid at **2T up until 05:00**. Any hours worked **from 05:00** are at the normal Hourly Rate, and **any call from 05:00 is treated as a normal working day**.

For the avoidance of doubt, a pre-dawn call is **not treated as Night Work** and **does not attract a Night Work Premium**.

### 5.2 Night Work

**(a) Night Work Definition.** Night Work is those **shooting hours which continue after midnight**, or **any unit call between midnight and 03:00**.

For the avoidance of doubt:
- **(i)** provided the camera is wrapped by midnight, wrap by crew members appropriate to their position undertaken after midnight **shall not be considered Night Work**; and
- **(ii)** where a department is asked to work overnight **for reasons other than shooting**, that department **shall receive the Night Work Premium**.

**(b) Night Work Premium.** Crew members taking part in Night Work — **including dailies** — receive a **£20.00 premium** for each **night actually worked**.

**(c) Turnaround Day.** All crew members, including dailies, required to work a scheduled period of Night Work are paid for **one turnaround day at their standard contractual daily rate** at the end of the scheduled period of Night Work. For dailies, a turnaround day is paid at the end of any scheduled period of Night Work — either consecutive nights, or after each individual stand-alone night. One turnaround day is **also** paid, including for dailies, if during the course of the week **the schedule begins with Night Work and ends on days or split days**.

**(d) Scheduling of Blocks of Five and Six Nights of Work.**

**(i)** If consecutive nights are scheduled, it is usual that they will be in **blocks of 5 nights**, with a rest period between each week of **48 + 11 hours**. However, if producers schedule consecutive nights in **6 night blocks**, this may only be done **twice in succession** — thereafter 5 night blocks and 6 night blocks **must alternate**, and there can be no more 6 night blocks in succession. For clarity, there **can** be 5 night blocks in succession.

| Transition | Minimum rest period |
|---|---|
| 6 night → 6 night | 24 + 11 hours |
| 6 night → 5 night | 24 + 11 hours |
| 5 night → 6 night | 48 + 11 hours |
| 5 night → 5 night | 48 + 11 hours |

**(ii)** If the schedule moves from a week of **6 consecutive nights** of work to day work, the crew member should be given a minimum rest period of **24 hours + 11 hours**, **in addition to** the turnaround day payment under §5.2(c).

**(iii)** If the schedule moves from a week of **5 consecutive nights** of work to day work, the crew member should be given a minimum rest period of **48 hours**, **in addition to** the turnaround day payment under §5.2(c).

### 5.3 Broken Turnarounds

Producers should endeavour to give crew members **eleven hours' turnaround** between the individual crew member's **wrap to their call** (the **Turnaround Period**). In the event of any shorter turnaround:

**(a)** the producer should wherever possible allow the crew member to take an **equivalent period of compensatory rest**, and shall in any event afford such protection as may be appropriate to safeguard the crew member's health and safety; and

**(b)** where a crew member is **required to work** during the Turnaround Period (**Broken Turnaround**), they should be paid at **1.5T** for any such time, accruing in **30 minute increments** and pro-rated accordingly, subject to an overall cap of **£45 per hour (or £22.50 per 30 minute increment)**.

### 5.4 Meal Breaks

**(a) SWD and SCWD.** During principal photography, crew members should be generally entitled to take their lunch break **no later than 6 hours after unit call**.

**(b)** If **shooting** crew members are asked by the producers to:

- **(i) delay** the lunch break — a penalty is paid in **15 minute increments at the crew member's standard Hourly Rate** for the period of delay, pro-rated for partial hours; or
- **(ii) curtail** the lunch break — a penalty is paid in **15 minute increments at the crew member's camera Overtime rate** for the period curtailed, pro-rated for partial hours and **capped at 1 hour on a SWD and 30 minutes on a SCWD**.

If **non-shooting** crew are asked by the producers to shorten their lunch break from one hour, then infringed time is paid in **30 minute increments at 1.5T**.

For the avoidance of doubt, the Head of Department for non-shooting crew should manage their department so crew members are able to take their lunch break, save as requested above. Where such a HOD wishes to curtail or delay the lunch break, **prior approval must be sought from the Unit Production Manager**, and the penalties above will apply.

**(c) Continuous Working Day (CWD).** Food will be provided **from 4.5 hours after the unit call** and will run for **two hours**.

**(d) Semi-Continuous Working Day (SCWD).** Food will be provided **from 4.5 hours after the unit call** and will run for **two hours**. During this time crew should be given a **30-minute break**.

**(e) General.** Food will be served during the **third hour of any Camera Overtime**.

### 5.5 Rest Breaks

The producers and HODs will work together to provide an additional **20-minute break** for individual crew members where they work **in excess of 12 hours** when shooting a **CWD or SCWD**.

### 5.6 Bank Holidays

**(a)** Where a crew member is contracted over a period which includes a **United Kingdom official public holiday**, or a **foreign official public holiday where work takes place abroad** (a **Bank Holiday**), but is **not actually required to work** that day, the crew member is entitled to receive their **daily fee** for that Bank Holiday.

**(b)** If required to work on a Bank Holiday, the crew member is entitled to be paid at **2T** during that Bank Holiday — whether working on a weekly or longer term basis, or engaged as a daily.

### 5.7 Late Cancellation of Dailies working days

Producers may only cancel the engagement of dailies **previously confirmed** (rather than merely pencilled) **without payment** of their daily rate if the producer or HOD gives notice **by 3pm the working day preceding** that daily's call. Notice may be by any means of recorded communication such as text, email and WhatsApp. **Cancellation after 3pm means the full daily rate is payable.**

### 5.8 Late Cancellation of Dailies working nights

Producers may only cancel the engagement of dailies previously confirmed **without payment** if the producer gives notice **by 3am the working night preceding** that daily's call. Notice may be by any means of recorded communication. **Cancellation after 3am means the full daily rate is payable.**

> Crew other than dailies should refer to their contract for terms relating to late cancellation.

### 5.9 Box rental for Dailies

Where box rental is required for the role of a daily, a **pro rata box rental fee** shall be agreed — equivalent, pro-rata, to the **weekly box rental fee** that would be payable if they had been engaged on a weekly basis.

## 6 — Travel and Bases

### 6.1 Definitions and Terminology

**(a)** Unless otherwise defined, the **Production Base** is the place where the production is carrying out the **majority of its work**.

**(b)** The producers may nominate a **different Base for each of pre-production, production and/or post production**.

**(c)** The producers may nominate one Base as above, but may **call to any other Recognised Studio** for the purposes of an individual working at that Recognised Studio.

**(d) Recognised Studios** are: West London, Shepperton, Pinewood, Leavesden, Elstree, Ealing, Twickenham, Gillette, Longcross and 3 Mills. This list **will be regularly updated** as new studios are established. Where travel is required **between Recognised Studios in a single working day**, travel mileage is payable per §6.2(b) unless transport is provided.

**(e)** For the purposes of this Agreement:

- **(i) Non-Resident Location** — a place of work where an individual **can** be expected to travel to and from the Production Base each day
- **(ii) Resident Location** — a place of work **more than 50 road miles** from the Production Base
- **(iii) Overseas Location** — a place of work situated **outside the United Kingdom**
- **(iv)** All the above together are defined as a **Location**

### 6.2 Terms Applicable to Travel

**(a)** **No payments** for any travel between a crew member's **home and Base**, or home and a **Recognised Studio**.

**(b)** **Mileage** is paid at the then-applicable **HMRC rate** for travel required from the Production Base to a Non-Resident Location or a Resident Location, **unless transport is provided**.

**(c) Local Locations.** The contractual rate payable to all crew members **includes provision for up to 30 road miles travel at the beginning and 30 road miles at the end** of the working day, to and from a filming location, **without further payment**.

**(d) Paid Travel — Local Locations.** Travel time and distance shall be **stipulated in the Location Manager's official movement order**. **No payment for the first 30 road miles.** Travel **beyond 30 road miles (either way)** to or from a Location, from the Production Base or Recognised Studio, is paid at their agreed rate in **30 minute increments** (pro-rated accordingly), **capped at £45.00 per hour or £22.50 per 30 minutes**.

**(e) Distant/Overseas Locations.** When accommodated in a hotel at a UK Resident or Overseas Location, the same provisions apply: **30 road miles at the beginning and 30 road miles at the end** of the working day, to and from the hotel, is permissible **without further payment**.

**(f) Paid Travel — Distant/Overseas Locations.** Travel time and distance stipulated in the Location Manager's official movement order. **No payment for the first 30 road miles.** Travel beyond 30 road miles paid at their agreed rate in **30 minute increments** (pro-rated), **capped at £45.00 per hour or £22.50 per 30 minutes**.

**(g) Off-set Prep Crews**, including electrical rigging crews: any time spent travelling to and from the Production Base and any location is **deemed to form part of the normal working day and will not be compensated**. Any overtime worked at a Location **must be pre-approved**. If that overtime is travel, payment is **capped at £45.00 per hour** and paid in 30 minute increments, pro-rated accordingly.

**(h)** Travel time, where payable, is paid at the **contractual daily/hourly rate** and is **not subject to any uplifts or premiums** that may otherwise be payable — **notwithstanding that such travel may take place on a 6th day, 7th day or Bank Holiday**.

**(i)** Where the producer requires crew to travel to any UK or Overseas Location **on a day outside their contracted working week**, payment is made at the **contractual flat daily rate**, regardless of any changes to time zones and regardless of the day of travel.

**(j)** **Rest days** while at a Resident Location or Overseas Location are **not paid unless actually worked**. This needs to be pre-approved and will be paid at the appropriate rate.

**(k)** If a crew member **decides** to travel home on a rest day from a Resident or Overseas Location, **no payment** will be made — **unless the producer has instructed them to do so**.

## Appendix 1 — Complaints and Dispute Procedures

**1.** Both Pact and Bectu shall submit any differences or disputes arising solely in relation to the terms of this Agreement in accordance with this Disputes Procedure **in a timely manner**, and agree to use this procedure **and no other** until it is exhausted.

**2.** In the first instance, crew members should **informally** raise issues of concern with the HOD, Line Producer, the Head of Production or Production Executive as referred to in their contract. Where informal efforts fail, the matter may be referred to the disputes procedure below **if it relates to the terms of this Agreement**.

**3.** Where a dispute about the terms of this Agreement arises between crew member(s) and an employer, either party — or Bectu or Pact — shall **within seven days** of its cause arising invoke the following procedure:

**3.1** Every endeavour shall be made by the authorised representative of the employer, the authorised unit representative of Bectu (if any) and the crew member(s) concerned to **resolve the matter at the place at which it has arisen**. Failing resolution within **seventy two hours**:

**3.2** The matter is referred to an **industry panel** consisting of two representatives of Bectu, a representative of the employer, and a Pact nominee — **none of whom shall be those directly concerned** with the matter in dispute. This meeting is held **within one week** of either party receiving a written request. The **Chair alternates**. Each member including the Chair has **one vote**.

- If the decision is **unanimous**, it is **binding**
- If the panel cannot reach a unanimous decision and either party wishes to pursue the matter, then **within seventy two hours** either party may refer the matter to **ACAS** for conciliation, or by mutual agreement for arbitration
- If no reference is made to ACAS or elsewhere, or such reference fails to achieve an agreed or arbitrated outcome, **the Disputes Procedure is exhausted**

**4.** The employer and crew member agree to accept as **final and binding** any resolution reached under this procedure. **No stoppage of work or lock-out** shall take place until the procedure has been invoked and all stages exhausted. Whatever practice or custom was in operation before registration of the dispute shall continue until the procedures are exhausted.

**5.** References to periods of **twenty four and seventy two hours** are interpreted as **excluding Saturdays, Sundays and UK Bank or Public Holidays**.

## Appendix B — Open Items And Source Gaps

Things this rebuild does **not** contain, and points worth resolving before any calculation work.

### Missing from the source

**The overtime rate examples appendix.** §3.3(a) states that "the attached Appendix details examples of overtime rates". The only appendix in the published PDF is **Appendix 1 — Complaints and Dispute Procedures**. The worked overtime examples referenced are **not present in the document**. This is the film equivalent of the Section D gap in the TV agreement, and it matters more here because film has two overtime types with different increments and a non-pro-rated third hour.

### Ambiguities worth resolving

**1. OT trigger, elapsed vs worked.** §2.3 says all three shooting-day variations are "paid as 11 worked hours", but the structures differ: SWD is 11 worked + 1 lunch (12h elapsed), CWD is 10 working hours (10h elapsed), SCWD is 10 working hours + 30 min (10.5h elapsed). §3.1 puts overtime after hours "in excess of a SWD, CWD or SCWD, as applicable", after expiry of prep and wrap. The elapsed-clock reading above is the natural one, but unlike the TV agreement there are **no worked examples** to confirm it for CWD and SCWD.

**2. The prep and wrap allowance and the OT clock.** §3.2(a) gives the listed departments up to 30 minutes at each end "in addition to the 55 hours worked", with no overtime due, and §3.1 says overtime is payable "after expiry of the relevant Prep and Wrap". So for those departments the practical overtime trigger appears to be day-end **plus 30 minutes**. But "prep and wrap will not be aggregated across a day" means the unused 30 minutes at the start **cannot** be carried to the end. Whether an early start consumes the morning 30 minutes and still leaves the evening 30 minutes intact is the exact question a calculator has to answer, and the text does not spell it out.

**3. Camera overtime, third hour.** "From the 3rd hour overtime will not be pro-rated for partial hours." The natural reading is that once you enter the third hour, that hour bills in full — so 2h05m of camera overtime bills as 3 hours. Whether the fourth and subsequent hours behave the same way is implied but not stated.

**4. Minimum Camera Overtime Rate scope.** §3.3(b) applies the £25/hr floor to **shooting** crew members only. §4.3(b) then says all 6th and 7th day hourly rates are "subject to the Minimum Camera Overtime Rate", without restating the shooting-crew limitation. Whether a non-shooting crew member gets the £25 floor on a 6th day is unclear.

**5. Delayed lunch at 1T.** §5.4(b)(i) pays a **delayed** lunch at the standard Hourly Rate, while §5.4(b)(ii) pays a **curtailed** lunch at the camera Overtime rate. This is a significant divergence from the TV agreement, where both pay at the Overtime Rate. Worth confirming against a real payslip before building anything on it.

**6. Broken Turnaround cap vs Maximum Overtime Rate.** Broken Turnaround is capped at **£45/hr** (§5.3(b)), and paid travel is capped at the same **£45/hr** (§6.2(d), (f), (g)). These are separate from and lower than the **£81.82/hr** Maximum Overtime Rate. Three different ceilings operate in this agreement.

**7. Currency of the figures.** The rate floors and ceilings — £25, £81.82, £45, £20, £3,000 — date from the 5 April 2021 amendment. There is no indexation clause in the text. Confirm whether a later amendment has revised them before relying on these numbers.

**8. Construction is out of scope.** §1.2 excludes construction crew entirely. Their terms are governed elsewhere.
