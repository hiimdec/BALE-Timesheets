APA crew rates 2026, what changes on 1 September

TimeMachine

On 1 September 2026 the APA rate card is replaced by a new one. It raises the basic day rate for most roles by roughly three per cent, and keeps each role in the same overtime grade at the same coefficient. Those are the two things the card sets; everything else your pay depends on comes from the terms, not the rate table.

What actually changes

It is not a flat three per cent. It is about three per cent on the basic day rate, rounded to the nearest pound, so the real change lands somewhere between 2.90% and 3.11% depending on your role. A gaffer goes from £568 to £585. A props assistant from £310 to £319, which is the smallest rise at 2.90%. The roles on £386 go to £398, the largest at 3.11%. In every case it is a few pounds a day.

Four roles do not change at all: the lighting trainee stays at £250, and the three rigging rates, which the app takes from the Bectu figures rather than the card, do not move either. Everyone else goes up. You can see every role, both cards side by side, on the current APA rates page.

What the card sets, and what it does not

The card sets two things: the basic day rates, and each role's overtime grade. The overtime coefficients are unchanged, so every role stays in the grade it was in, at the same multiplier. Everything else your pay depends on, the penalties, the meal and mileage allowances, the break and turnaround thresholds, TimeMachine works out from the terms rather than from the rate table, so they are not part of what this card changes. The way a day rate becomes an hourly rate, a tenth of the day rate, is part of that same structure.

The same day, both cards

Take a gaffer, comfortably inside Grade II on both cards, so the overtime coefficient is 1.25 either way. Call at 08:00, wrap at 22:00, so three hours of overtime after the eleven hour basic day.

On the 2025 card the day rate is £568. The hourly rate is a tenth of that, £56.80, and the overtime hour is 1.25 times it, £71.00. Three overtime hours is £213.00, so the day comes to £781.00.

On the 2026 card the day rate is £585. The hourly rate is £58.50, and the overtime hour is 1.25 times it, which is £73.125. The published card rounds that to the pound, £73, so three overtime hours is £219.00 and the day comes to £804.00.

The rise is £23.00 a day: £17 on the day rate, and £6 across the three overtime hours at the card's rounded rate. That overtime hour is worth a word. Before rounding it is £73.125. TimeMachine keeps the pennies by default, so a day like this comes out a few pennies above the card's total. It can also be set to round each rate to the pound, which matches the card, or to round each line up in your favour, so your own figure may land a little either side of £804.

A job that spans 1 September

TimeMachine resolves the rate card once per production, from the production's earliest day. So a shoot that starts in August and runs into September currently uses the August card, the 2025 one, for every day of it, including the days on or after the 1st.

This is how the app behaves today. It is not a statement about what the terms require, and it is not a promise that it will stay this way, it is simply what the current version does. If you want the 2026 card to apply, the production's first day needs to be on or after 1 September 2026.

Jobs you have already costed

A production you set up under the 2025 card keeps those rates. A new card does not reach back and re-rate a job you costed months ago. The rates were fixed onto the production when you built it, and they stay put unless you move its dates across the change.

Where the figures come from

The rates here are from the APA Recommended Crew Terms. For the full card, see the APA's published document.
