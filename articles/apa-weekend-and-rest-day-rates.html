APA Saturday, Sunday and rest day rates explained

TimeMachine · Published 24 August 2026

Weekend work is where most APA invoicing mistakes happen. The multipliers are simple on paper, but they interact with day types, overtime and midnight rules in ways that are easy to get wrong at 11pm on a Sunday. Here's the full picture.

Saturday shoot days

A basic working day on a Saturday is charged at 1.5 times your basic daily rate, with a minimum 10 hour call. Overtime after the basic working day is at 1.5 times your basic hourly rate, regardless of your weekday overtime grade. The weekday grade system (1.5, 1.25 or 1.0 depending on your rate) only applies Monday to Friday. On a Saturday, everyone's overtime is time and a half.

Overtime after midnight is still triple time, even on a Saturday, and continues at triple until wrap if you go past 5am.

Sunday and bank holiday shoot days

A basic working day on a Sunday, bank holiday or statutory holiday is charged at double your basic hourly rate for all hours worked, minimum 10 hour call. Overtime is at double your basic hourly rate. After midnight is still triple.

Night shoots: no double double

A night shoot (call between 5pm and 5am) is double your basic hourly rate for all hours worked, minimum 10 hours, with no overtime rate and no triple time after midnight. That's true on any day of the week. A night shoot that starts on a Saturday or Sunday is still double, not triple or quadruple. There is no double double.

Non shooting days on weekends

  • Prep, recce, construction and strike days: on a Saturday, 8 hours at 1.5 times your basic hourly rate, overtime after 8 hours at the same 1.5 rate. On a Sunday or bank holiday, the same shape at double.
  • Pre-light days: on a Saturday, 8 hours at 1.5 times your basic hourly rate with a lunch hour, overtime after 9 hours from call. Sunday or bank holiday, the same at double.
  • These weekend rules were unchanged by the September 2026 terms, including for prep days, whose weekday rules did change.

Rest days: flat fee, any day of the week

A rest day is when production asks you to remain on location but no filming takes place. It's charged as a flat fee equal to your basic daily rate, with no overtime, no penalties and no meal compensation, and the same flat fee applies whichever day of the week it falls on. A rest day on a Sunday is not doubled.

A rest day is not the same as a day off. If production hasn't asked you to remain on location, it's just a gap in the schedule and nothing is payable.

Travel days: single time, even on a Sunday

Travel time is always paid at single time, whatever the day. A travel day on a Saturday, Sunday or bank holiday is charged at your basic hourly rate with a minimum 5 hour call, exactly as on a weekday. This surprises people every year. The weekend multipliers apply to working days, not travel.

Breaks and penalties still apply

Break provisions are the same every day of the week, including weekends and bank holidays: the first break within 5.5 hours of call, the second within 5.5 hours of the end of the first, with the usual penalties if they're late, curtailed or missed.

A worked example

Say your basic daily rate is £400, so your basic hourly rate is £40.

  • Saturday shoot, call 8am, wrap 9pm (13 hours): the day is 1.5 × £400 = £600 for the 11 hour basic working day, then 2 hours of overtime at 1.5 × £40 = £60 per hour, so £720 total.
  • The same day on a Sunday: double your basic hourly rate for all hours worked. Twelve working hours at £80 = £960.
  • A rest day that weekend: £400 flat, either day.

Who this doesn't apply to

Production Managers, Production Assistants and Runners sit outside these clauses. They have their own weekend treatment: overtime on shoot days only, with the Saturday day at 1.5 times and Sunday, bank holiday and night shoot days at double, calculated between main unit call and tail lights.
