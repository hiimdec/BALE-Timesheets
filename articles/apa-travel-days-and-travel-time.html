APA travel days and travel time: what are you actually owed?

TimeMachine · Published 24 August 2026

Travel is the part of the APA terms crew most often undercharge, because it's really three separate things with three separate rules: travel days, travel time on a working day, and mileage. Mileage is money for your car and we've covered it separately. This article covers the other two, which are money for your time.

Travel days: minimum 5 hours at single time

A travel day is a non shooting day spent moving to or from location. It's paid at your basic hourly rate for the hours travelled, with a minimum 5 hour call. It's always single time, regardless of the day of the week: a Sunday travel day pays exactly the same as a Tuesday one. The weekend multipliers never apply to travel.

One useful knock on rule: after a travel day where the crew stays in a hotel, the next working day is calculated base to base, hotel to hotel. Your day starts when you leave the hotel and ends when you're back, not when you reach set. This applies to all location shoots where crew are put up overnight.

Travel time on a working day: the 11 hour rule and the first hour

If you travel to a shoot and back on the same day you work, travel time is payable at your basic hourly rate, with two big qualifications:

  1. Nothing is payable at all unless your travel time plus working time comes to at least 11 hours. A 9 hour day with 45 minutes each way doesn't reach the threshold, so the travel is unpaid.
  2. The first hour of the outward journey and the first hour of the homeward journey are deducted. You're charging for travel beyond an hour each way, not door to door.

For London based production companies, the starting point for measuring travel is the APA's address in W1F 9SE, not your home.

A worked example: a Lighting Technician on the 2026 card maximum, basic hourly rate £46, works a full 11 hour shoot day with 1.5 hours of travel each way. Total time is 14 hours, comfortably over the threshold. Deduct the first hour of each journey and 0.5 hours each way is payable: 1 hour at £46. Not a fortune on one day, but across a week on a distant location it adds up, and it's yours under the terms.

Equipment and personnel collection

If production asks you to collect equipment or other crew from any address other than your home, that time counts as working time on a base to base basis. Picking up a genny, collecting kit from a rental house, or driving another crew member in: all working time.

From 1 September 2026 there is one new exclusion: base to base does not apply to equipment hired from the crew member. If production is hiring the kit from you, driving your own kit to set no longer automatically counts as working time under this clause, and the terms don't provide a replacement formula. If you supply your own kit, agree collection and delivery terms as part of the kit deal up front. Full details in our September 2026 changes article.

Flying to location

Air travel has its own protections. If your flight time is over 4 hours, there's no shooting on the day of the flight. If the scheduled flight time is over 8 hours, there's no shooting until 24 hours after arrival. Both subject to exceptional circumstances, but the default is rest before you work.

Who this doesn't apply to

The travel clauses don't apply to Production Managers, Production Assistants or Runners.
