# TimeMachine — Marketing Plan

The execution layer. `GO_TO_MARKET.md` decides the *strategy and sequence* (free now, Pro later, channels in priority order, when to charge). This is the *how*: what you say, what you make, and how you actually run each channel. Read them together.

## Positioning — what TimeMachine *is*, in one breath

**The hook:** "Know exactly what you're owed — APA pay worked out in seconds, and invoiced in a tap."

**The promise:** crew get short-changed and confused by APA rules all the time. TimeMachine gives you certainty about your own money — and the paperwork to claim it.

**Why you, not Rate** (lead with these, in this order):
1. **Built by crew, for crew** — you're one of them, not a software company guessing at the rules.
2. **APA-accurate to the letter** — every day type, OT grade, penalty, bank-holiday multiplier, cancellation tier. This is your moat; the audit you just did is the proof.
3. **Invoicing included** — the thing Rate makes you pay extra for.
4. **Free to start, and totally private** — no account, no cloud, nothing leaves your phone.

## Audience — and how the message shifts

- **Individual crew (the many).** Message: *"Stop guessing your pay. Know your worth, get paid right — free."* Emotional core: confidence and your own money.
- **Best Boys & HoDs (the multipliers).** Message: *"Run your whole department's times and invoices in one place."* This is where Best Boy mode earns its keep — and one HoD brings a whole crew.
- **Downstream recipients (production accountants, coordinators).** They *receive* your PDFs. Every clean, branded timesheet that lands on their desk is a quiet credibility ad — they start recognising the name.

## Messaging principles (the do's and don'ts)

- Lead with **utility and honesty**, never "buy my app." In tight crew communities, a sell smells wrong instantly.
- **Give value first** (see the content strategy) so you've earned the right to mention the app.
- **Show, don't tell** — the breakdown and the PDF do the selling. A screenshot of someone's real OT being caught is worth a paragraph of copy.
- **Contribute, don't spam.** Be a real member of the group who happens to have built something useful.

## Your sharpest, cheapest lever: education content

You understand the APA rules better than almost anyone — the app *is* that knowledge encoded. So **teach it.** Short, genuinely useful pieces that crew will share among themselves:

- "How APA overtime actually works (and the grade that quietly cuts your rate)"
- "Are you being underpaid? Five things to check on your next timesheet"
- "Bank-holiday and weekend rates, explained"
- "Cancellation fees: what you're owed when a job drops"

This does three jobs at once: it's endlessly shareable in crew circles, it positions **TimeMachine as the APA authority**, and it pulls people to the app without ever feeling like an ad. Post in the groups, on the landing page, maybe a simple blog. **Cadence: one genuinely useful thing every week or two** — sustainable for a solo founder, and it compounds.

## Assets to make (production checklist)

- **App Store screenshots** with the PDF and the breakdown front and centre — the output is the wow.
- **A 60-second demo video:** Best Boy enters a day → four crew pre-fill → a clean PDF drops out. That single clip sells the whole thing; use it everywhere.
- **The founder story** (below) written once, reused everywhere.
- **One-line pitch**, with a couple of variants to test.
- **Landing page** (`timemachineapp.co.uk/about`) — hook, the demo video, screenshots, the founder story, a download button. Live before launch.
- **3–5 testimonials** pulled from your beta testers.

## The founder story — your single most powerful asset

In a community this tight, *who built it* matters as much as what it does. Write a short, honest version of: *"I'm a working crew member. I got sick of squinting at the APA rules to work out what I was actually owed — and watching mates get underpaid because nobody checks. So I built the tool I wanted."* That story anchors the landing page, the Facebook posts, and any press pitch. It's the thing a software company can never copy.

## Launch campaign — concentrate it so it looks like momentum

**Pre-launch (during the TestFlight beta):**
- Tell your network it's coming; gather testimonials.
- Line up your launch-day reviewers (5+ ready to post the moment it's live).
- A simple "coming to the App Store" teaser post with the demo video.

**Launch week (a coordinated burst, not a trickle):**
- Day one: beta testers post their App Store reviews.
- You post the founder story + demo video to your network and one or two crew Facebook groups.
- The BECTU branch message goes out.
- Landing page live.
- Concentrating it into a few days makes it *read* as momentum, which itself attracts people.

**Post-launch (sustain):**
- The education drumbeat (every 1–2 weeks).
- HoD/Best Boy outreach, one at a time.
- Reply to **every** review and piece of feedback — in a small community, being responsive is marketing.

## Per-channel playbook

- **Best Boys / HoDs (top priority):** approach personally, offer to set it up *for* them on a real upcoming job, and show the department-times-and-invoices value. One yes seeds a whole crew.
- **Your own network:** direct, specific asks ("would you try this on your next job and tell me what breaks?"), and make sharing one tap.
- **BECTU (Sparks branch first):** use your template message; offer a 10-minute demo at a branch meeting; ask for a newsletter mention. Trusted, targeted, warm.
- **Crew Facebook groups:** post the founder story, not an ad. Be present in the comments. Drop the education pieces here. Don't hard-sell — let the demo and the story do it.
- **Press (later, for credibility):** a tight pitch built on the founder story + the demo video, to the trade titles (Definition, ProVideo Coalition, BECTU magazine).

## Measuring it (keep it light)

Attribution is fuzzy with no backend, so use rough signals, not a dashboard you won't maintain:
- Downloads and App Store rating/reviews (your clearest public signal).
- A noticeable bump after a post tells you which channel landed.
- Once Pro is live: conversion rate and churn.
Track just enough to know what's working and do more of it.

## A realistic solo-founder rhythm

Don't try to run this like a marketing department — you'll burn out. A sustainable weekly rhythm: **one outreach (an HoD or a branch), one piece of value (a post or a tip), and genuine presence in one group.** Small and consistent beats a big launch push followed by silence.

*(Marketing judgement, not gospel — adapt as you see what your community actually responds to.)*
