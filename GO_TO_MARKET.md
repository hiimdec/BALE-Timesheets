# TimeMachine — Go-to-Market Plan

A working plan, not a manifesto. Sits alongside `V5_KICKOFF.md` (the Pro features below are largely the V5 backlog) and `WORKING_METHOD.md`.

## North star

You're in a **land-grab**, not a monetise phase. With ~0 users against Rate's ~1,000, your scarcest assets are *users and trust*, not revenue. So: **free now to grow, Pro later to monetise the heaviest users.** Free is your wedge against an established paid incumbent — "it's free, just try it" spreads through a crew WhatsApp group in a way "£30/year" never will.

Three things only you have, that the plan leans on:
- **You're actually crew.** An authentic recommendation inside the community beats any funnel.
- **Invoicing is built in** — Rate gates it behind a premium tier. That's your switching incentive.
- **Near-zero running costs** (no backend) — better margins per user than Rate, and you can afford to be free for a long time.

---

## The model: freemium, sequenced

Free core that spreads → a Pro layer that monetises power users once there's a base worth monetising. Don't paywall a thin base; it strangles the growth that's the whole point of this phase.

---

## Phased plan

### Phase 0 — Foundations (now, in parallel)
- Get onto TestFlight *(in progress)*.
- **Enrol in the App Store Small Business Program** → 15% commission instead of 30%. Do this before any paid tier; it roughly halves Apple's cut.
- Decide the free/Pro split on paper (below) even before Pro ships — it shapes what you build.
- Make the "Made with TimeMachine" export footer a tasteful, tappable link to the App Store / site.

### Phase 1 — Beta (TestFlight)
**Goal:** real feedback, first advocates, launch-day reviews lined up.
- 20–30 known crew on external TestFlight.
- Log friction, fix blockers.
- Line up 3–5 enthusiastic testers as future advocates / mini case studies, and ask them to be ready to post an App Store review *on launch day*.

### Phase 2 — Public launch (free, on the App Store)
**Goal:** live, free, and easy to recommend.
- Launch free, everything unlocked for now.
- Day one: tell the beta testers and collect those reviews — 5+ early reviews matter for credibility and ranking.
- Landing page live (`timemachineapp.co.uk/about`); privacy + terms pages up.
- Start the highest-leverage outreach (HoDs/Best Boys — see channels).

### Phase 3 — Grow the base (still free)
**Goal:** a few hundred active users; the word-of-mouth engine running.
- Work the channels in priority order (below).
- Lean on the viral loop — every shared PDF advertises the app to exactly the right people.
- **Watch usage:** which features do people actually lean on? That data decides the Pro split.
- Gather reviews and testimonials continuously.

**→ Exit trigger to Phase 4 (the "when to charge" gate). Move only when all are true:**
- A few hundred *regular* users (a paywall won't kill momentum).
- Usage data clearly shows the power features people rely on.
- Those Pro features are polished enough to be worth money.
- You have social proof (reviews, testimonials) in hand.

### Phase 4 — Introduce Pro (monetise)
**Goal:** monetise power users without choking growth.
- Ship the Pro tier (the V5 features: accounting exports, Best Boy/team, AI call-sheet reader, kit library, advanced stats).
- **Grandfather your early users** — lock them on a generous free tier or a permanent low price. They're your advocates; this rewards them and avoids the "but it used to be free" backlash that sinks goodwill.
- Keep the free tier genuinely useful (core calculator) so growth continues alongside revenue.
- Price at or just under Rate; annual billing (lower churn than monthly).

### Phase 5 — Sustain & scale
**Goal:** climb toward Rate's 1,000+ and beyond, where the economics get real.
- Iterate Pro on actual demand, not guesses.
- Keep the community presence; use press for credibility.
- Track conversion, churn and average revenue per user; adjust price/split as you learn.

## Free vs Pro split

**Free (the growth engine — must be genuinely useful alone):**
- The full APA pay calculator, all day types, breakdowns
- Basic timesheet PDF
- Single-user
- **Basic invoice generation** — keep this free; it's your sharpest wedge against Rate, who charges for it

**Pro (~£25–30/year — the professional workflow):**
- Accounting/invoice exports (Xero, QuickBooks, CSV, Excel)
- Best Boy / team management
- AI call-sheet reader
- Kit library
- Advanced stats

**Principle:** free has to spread; Pro has to be obviously worth it to the heaviest users — especially Best Boys, who'd happily expense it.

## Acquisition channels (priority order)

1. **Best Boys & HoDs — start here.** Highest leverage by far: one gaffer or Best Boy using it for their department puts it in front of their whole crew at once. Best Boy mode exists precisely for this. Target: five HoDs genuinely using it.
2. **Your own network** — crew you've worked with. Your TestFlight 30 and your first reviews. Highest trust, lowest effort.
3. **BECTU branches**, Sparks/Lighting first — newsletters, meetings, branch groups. You already have a template message.
4. **Crew Facebook groups** (UK Film Crew, UK Sparks, Camera Department UK). Post *after* you've got testimonials and a polished build, so it lands as a recommendation, not an ad.
5. **Press** (Definition, ProVideo Coalition, BECTU magazine) — later, for credibility, not volume.

**The built-in viral loop:** every timesheet and invoice carries the "Made with TimeMachine" footer, so each one a user sends to a producer, accountant or fellow crew member is free, perfectly-targeted advertising. Make it tappable and let it compound.

## Pricing & economics (reference)

- **Enrol in the Small Business Program** → you keep 85%, not 70%.
- **Pro ~£25–30/year.** Don't drop below ~£20 — underpricing signals "hobby" in a professional tool, and the value (catching underpayment worth hundreds per job) easily justifies £30.
- **Take-home after Apple's 15%, at £30/year:**
  - 200 paying → £5,100
  - 300 → £7,650
  - 500 → £12,750
  - 1,000 (Rate's scale) → £25,500
- **Freemium reality:** paying users are a *fraction* of total. At a 20–30% conversion, 200 paying means ~700–1,000 total users. Size your top-of-funnel accordingly.

---

## Honest expectations

- This is a **slow, steady build**, not a spike — niche word-of-mouth always is, and Rate's thousand took time too.
- **Freemium conversion is low** — single digits to maybe ~15% even for a strong pro tool. Plan to that.
- At ~200 paying / £30, this is **meaningful side-income (~£5k/yr), not a salary.** The lever that moves it is *volume*, not price — the ceiling on price is low in a freelance market. 200 is the first milestone, not the destination.
- Your edge isn't a clever growth hack — it's being one of them, recommending it in their own rooms.
- *(Not financial advice — a sensible starting plan to adapt as you learn.)*
