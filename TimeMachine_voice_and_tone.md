# TimeMachine — Voice & Tone

> **Scope note:** this document overlaps BRAND.md's territory - it covers VOICE and copy (who TimeMachine sounds like), where BRAND.md covers visual identity (colour, type, layout). A future session may want to fold the two into one brand file; until then, BRAND.md stays the single source for off-app visual surfaces and this is the copy-voice reference.

> Reference for all user-facing copy. Voice is who TimeMachine *is* and never changes; tone is how that voice dials up or down for the moment.

## Who TimeMachine is

The best boy you'd actually want on set: razor-sharp about your money, a good laugh about everything else. Most film people are gloriously unserious — and brilliant at the job. That's the vibe. Competence you can trust, handed over with warmth and a bit of banter.

> **In one line:** Dead serious about your pay. Doesn't take itself too seriously.

## The person behind it

If TimeMachine were crew, it's the one you book again:

- **Friendly and genuinely nice** — never moody. Off days happen; it doesn't take them out on you.
- **Kind and helpful** — shows you a better way without ego, even if you've been doing this longer than it has.
- **Hardworking and tidy** — on top of the detail so you don't have to be.
- **Good banter** — goes from a laugh to "right, this needs doing" in a heartbeat.
- **Quietly, seriously organised** — the best-boy instinct: everyone looked after, nothing missed, and when something slips, *what happened and how do we not do that again.*

## Voice vs tone (the method)

- **Voice** = the qualities that are always there.
- **Tone** = how those same qualities are turned up or down for the situation. None is ever turned fully off — it's just adjusting the balance.

**Always on (never dialed down):**
- **Clear & accurate** — TimeMachine's non-negotiable, the way "smart" was always-on in Apple's example. Doubly so around money. Concise comes with it: say it, move on.

**The four dials (tone = where these sit for a given screen):**
- **Confident** — how sure-footed it sounds. (Steadiest dial; rarely dips.)
- **Warm** — how friendly and personal.
- **Funny** — how much banter and character. (The widest-swinging dial.)
- **Helpful** — how much it guides and explains.

## The two presets you'll reach for most

- **Money & consequences (serious):** Clear maxed · Helpful up · Confident up (reassuring authority) · Warm eased back · Funny ~off.
  *Rates, totals, penalties, invoices, errors, deleting data — anything that costs you.*
- **Everything else (playful):** Funny up · Warm up · Confident steady · Helpful as needed · Clear always on.
  *Empty states, stats, onboarding, celebrations, tips, nudges.*

Even a serious error stays a touch warm; even a joke stays clear. Never all the way off.

## Writing any line — four steps

1. **Which situation, where do the four dials sit?** (Usually one of the two presets.)
2. **Write the plainest version of the message first.**
3. **Dial in personality only where it earns its place** — never sacrifice usefulness for a joke.
4. **Read it aloud:** does this sound like the person above, in this situation? If not, rewrite.

## Guardrails

- **Clarity wins.** If the joke fogs the meaning, cut the joke.
- **Never funny at the user's expense** — never about their money being wrong, never sarcastic *at* them.
- **Confident, not cocky. Helpful, not "well, actually."** The senior-crew test: would this line wind up someone who's done this 20 years? Rewrite it.
- **No moods.** Errors aren't a telling-off — "here's what happened, here's the fix." Never blame.
- **Talk like crew** — call, wrap, turnaround, kit, BB — but no jargon for its own sake, and nothing that leaves a newcomer out.
- **Easy on "please" and "sorry"** — earned, not filler.

## It in practice (illustrative — real lines get written in the pass)

**Serious preset:**
- Invoice sent → `Invoice TM-INV-032 sent to Jolene's Bakery.` — full stop, no exclamation, no joke.
- Penalty → `Short turnaround: under 11 hours before your next call, so this day's on the broken-turnaround rate.`
- Destructive → `Delete this shoot? Its days and invoices go too, and there's no getting them back.`

**Playful preset:**
- Empty invoices → `No invoices yet. Go earn some — we'll handle the maths.`
- Stat → `£1,240 this month. That's about 310 of Jolene's sausage rolls, if you're counting.`
- Onboarding → `Right, let's get you set up. Two minutes, then you never do timesheet maths again.`
- Long-day wrap → `That's a wrap. 14 hours — go home.`

## How we use this

The voice is the constant; every screen is just *which preset*, and a read-aloud check. We go surface by surface — Settings labels and explainers first, then empty states, errors, onboarding, stats, invoicing, notifications. For each line: where do the dials sit, and does it sound like the person.

## House rules (mechanics)
- **British English throughout.** -ise not -ize, -our not -or, -re not -er, double-l in inflections (cancelled, travelled, labelled), "maths", "grey", "postcode", "licence" (noun) / "license" (verb). Code and CSS (e.g. `color`) are exempt — this is user-facing copy only.
- **No em dashes (—)** — they read as an AI tell. Use a spaced hyphen ( - ), a comma, brackets, or a semicolon.
- **En dashes (–) only for number/time ranges** (09:00–17:30, 6–4 days) — nowhere else.
- **Brackets, hyphens, and semicolons** are all welcome.
- **Sentence case** for buttons, labels, and controls. Acronyms (VAT, APA, BDR, IBAN) and proper/brand names keep their caps.
- **Errors:** what happened + the fix, plainly; no blame, no jargon, easy on "please"/"sorry"; raw error strings go to the console, not the user.
